# Cascade

**Category:** Arcade
**Tier:** TBD
**Status:** Disabled pending rework (#3033); hidden in store builds by #3001

## How to Play

Cascade is a physics-based piece-drop game. Pieces fall from the top of the screen into a bin. When two pieces of the same tier collide they merge into a higher-tier piece, scoring points. You win by making the capstone (Pumpkin / Milky Way, #3920); you lose when the bin overflows.

### Piece Progression

Pieces evolve through **11 fixed tiers** (0-10, #3920; 12 before). The lowest-tier pieces are droppable (tiers 0-5); higher tiers are only reachable via merging. Two pieces of tier n (n < 10) merge into tier n + 1; two tier-9 pieces make the **capstone** (tier 10, Pumpkin / Milky Way), which is never dropped (`MAX_TIER` = 10). **Making the capstone is the win** (#3920): see [The win](#the-win-3920). #3920 removed the Peach (Fruits) and the Moon (Cosmos), and made the Apple green; their drawings are kept as [retired pieces](#retired-pieces-3920).

**One table.** `frontend/src/game/cascade/tiers.ts` is the only place a tier's radius, score, density, droppable flag and name keys are defined. The engine (`pieceDefs.ts`), the board renderer, the spawner and its lid ring, the next-piece preview, the theme selector, the dev panel and the i18n keys all read it. `theme/fruitSets.ts` keeps only per-set names, emoji, colours and sprite references. `tiers.test.ts` fails if any other module defines a per-tier radius, score or density.

- **Radii** (#3920, owner decision after playing #3904's V1, which felt too small): one constant ratio for the whole ladder, `radius(n) = 11.5 * k^n` with k = (98 / 11.5)^(1/10) = 1.23895, **unrounded**, so every tier-to-tier ratio is exactly k and the capstone is exactly 98. The Cherry is about 6.4 % of the bin's 360 px inner width across (the share in the owner's reference game), about 22 pt on a 390 pt phone (the board is about 0.97 pt per world unit); the Pumpkin stays about half the bin. (#3904's V1 was `5 * (98 / 5)^(n / 11)` on 12 tiers; #3697's ladder was `round(18 * (56 / 18)^(n / 10))`, 18 to 56, with the capstone at 63 off the curve.) Why 11 tiers and a single capstone: on 12 tiers the bigger cherry could not be won (0 of 100 skilled games; two capstones take 4,096 cherries' worth of material, each now about 5 times the area), and two-capstone wins on 10 or 11 tiers stayed at 4-24 %; issue #3920 has the measured variants.
- **Drawn radius** is 1.5 % inside the collider (`DRAW_INSET`, `tierDrawnRadius`, #3904): the last of the owner's overlap levers, see [Overlap](#overlap-3904). The board, the art bitmaps and the spawner draw at it; physics, the lid, spawning and merges use the radius.
- **Droppable tiers** are 0-5 (#3920). The largest (Apple / Neptune, r = 33.6) spawns with its top edge at y = 7.9, on the canvas.
- **Scores** double per tier: a merge into tier n scores `2^n`; the winning merge into the capstone scores its normal 1024, with no extra (#3920). These values are provisional: the final ones are set with the time-bonus story (#3700), so a win dominates a long losing run. There is no jackpot bonus.
- **Density** is planck.js's fixture density, `(r0 / r)^0.5` (#3697): mass grows as r^1.5 instead of r^2. On this ladder the capstone is 24.9 times a cherry (87 on #3904's, 6.5 on #3697's). #3904 tried flattening it (exponents 0.75 to 1.37, down to today's 6.5 : 1) as the owner's first overlap lever; see [Overlap](#overlap-3904) for why it stayed. Only the ratios matter.
- **Art.** All 22 pieces (11 fruits, 11 cosmos) are drawn in code, in `game/cascade/art/`; see [Art (#3698)](#art-3698). A tier whose art cannot be rasterised is drawn as a plain circle in its placeholder colour.
- **Names** are `cascade:pieces.<set>.<key>` in every locale (22 keys; #3920 removed `fruits.peach` and `cosmos.moon`; English copy in locales still to be translated, tracked in `scripts/i18n-english-copies.json`).

### Gameplay

- The current piece and the next queued piece are shown at the top of the screen
- The player taps or swipes to aim, then releases to drop
- Pieces roll and stack after landing — physics are fully simulated at 60 Hz
- Merges can chain: a merge may cause the new piece to immediately contact another of the same tier, triggering another merge
- Game over when the pile stays over the bin's lid for 3 seconds, or reaches the top of the board: see [Game over: the lid](#game-over-the-lid)
- The win: making the capstone (#3920). The merge scores as usual and play ends: no more drops. A short celebration plays, then the "You Win!" result card (#3699): see [The celebration and the win card](#the-celebration-and-the-win-card-3699).

### The win (#3920)

- **Rule.** Making the capstone is the win: the merge of two tier-9 pieces (Watermelon / Sun) into the capstone (tier 10, Pumpkin / Milky Way), by any merge path (contact, the 1.5 px merge-gap scan, chain reach). It is an ordinary merge (the `merge` event and its `score`, the capstone's 1024, no extra), then `{ type: "win", x, y, id }`: where the capstone was made, and its id. It replaced #3904's rule (two capstones touching, both burst, 2 x 2048), which #3920 removed: two capstones never merge and are not a win (only the dev panel or a restore can put two on the board).
- **The end of play (engine gate).** From the step of the win, `canDrop()` is false and `drop()` does nothing (so the screen's taps stop, and the spawner shows its waiting ghost), no other merge runs (one pending in the same step included) and the lid rule is off (no `gameOver` follows a win), and the score is final. The engine itself keeps stepping the physics when asked, so the capstone grows in. `hasWon()` reads it; `restore()` starts a new game. The engine owns the gate so the screen, the test seams and the sim all see the same end; the screen keeps its frame loop running after the `win` event until no piece is growing in (the capstone is made at its parents' size and grows in over `GROW_IN_STEPS`, about 0.27 s), plus 100 ms to settle (`game/cascade/winTail.ts`, at most 240 frames), then stops it, so the board stays on a full-size capstone (a background trip in that window pauses the tail and the resume finishes it, never a new loop); at the win it completes the session (outcome `win`, #3699), keeps the best and clears the saved game, saving nothing more (pause, background or drop), so a relaunch starts a new game and a won game is never restored. The test hook state carries `won`.
- **Why the engine and not the screen.** One gate, the one the screen already reads for taps (`canDrop`).

Now the screen flags the board once it holds a tier-9 piece (a merge into tier 9, a restored save, a set-up or a spawn), and `components/cascade/useCelebrationStyle.ts` builds the style in idle time (`requestIdleCallback`, 1.5 s deadline), never on the frame or gesture path; only games one merge from the win pay for it. A win before that has run builds it at once, after the win's commit. Measured on web (Chromium, CanvasKit): 10 ms at 1x, 50 ms at 3x; a `__DEV__` (and test-hook build) log prints each build's time on a device.
- **Clock.** `CascadeCelebration` (rendered by `GameResultModal` as its `celebration` at the win) steps a shared value on the UI thread, one frame at a time (`useFrameCallback`, `stepCelebrationClock`), each frame moving it at most `MAX_FRAME_STEP_MS` (two 60 Hz frames). It starts only when the board is ready to draw the celebration (`onCelebrationReady`: the game is won and the style is built, or cannot be, with no Skia), at the time since the win but never past the 400 ms grow-in (`celebrationStartMs`), so the card comes about 2.1 s after the celebration can be seen. The frame that reaches the card's time calls `done`; a JS timer (the card's time plus `FALLBACK_SLACK_MS`, 1 s) calls it if frames never get there. It is no Reanimated animation, so the OS Reduce Motion setting cannot shorten it; the calm visuals come from `reduceMotion`. `CascadeBoard`'s `useDerivedValue` closes over the clock, so Reanimated re-runs it (and re-records the Picture) on every step (`worklets.test.ts` checks the closure). `GameResultModal` mounts the card only after `done`, so the card never covers the animation (#2201); its 4 s safety net (`CELEBRATION_MAX_MS`, from the win) still shows the card if the board never becomes ready.
- **Why stepped, and the warm-up (second iOS demo on #3924).** In the iOS recording the screen redraws at about 60 fps once the celebration shows, so the Picture does follow the clock; but nothing is drawn for about 0.44 s before the first celebration frame, and that frame is already risen, dimmed and cheering, with the card about 2 s after the start. The first celebration frame stalled the UI thread (the first GPU use of the four 1.4x textures and the gradient, stroke and clip pipelines), and the wall-clock `withTiming` ran on through the stall past the rise, which is front-loaded (easeOutBack is 98 % there 160 ms into its 450 ms). Two fixes: the stepped clock (a stall only delays the celebration), and `warmCelebration`, which draws all of it as an invisible speck (each part at 1/255 alpha, the capstone at 2 %) from the moment the style is built (the pre-warm, during play) until the celebration starts, so the GPU work is done before it: 8 tiny draws a frame, only while a tier-9 piece is on the board.
- **Cost per frame** (`render/__tests__/drawCelebration.test.ts`): the board, plus at most 39 draws on Fruits and 117 on Cosmos (1 dim, the lit twinkles, 12 rays or 90 dots, 1 glow, 1 ring, 3-4 bitmaps). Nothing is allocated per frame (the Skia rects for the bitmaps, paths and shaders are made at the win; the paint and the pose are reused); no blur (#3641). No physics runs: the loop has stopped. Not yet measured on a device with the frame readout (#2960).
- **The card.** The shared `GameResultModal` with outcome `win`, as Blackjack's goal-reached result: "You Win!", the score (merge score + time bonus, the server's formula, #3700), New best when it is one, then Merge score, Time bonus, Time (play time, the screen's `PlayClock` at the win) and Best, View leaderboard, Play Again (a fresh game) and Home (`components/cascade/CascadeResultModal.tsx`). The card holds four stats, so the win card leaves out Merges. A loss keeps its card: outcome `ended` ("Game Over"), the score (the merge score), Best, Merges and Time, with no bonus line.
- **Background.** A trip to the background mid-celebration does not restart play (the win tail has ended or resumes the same tail) and the card shows once.
- **Not covered.** `GameResultModal` fades the card in (`animationType="fade"`) whatever Reduce Motion says; that is the shared modal, left as it is here.

### Game over: the lid

The lid is the line at `OVERFLOW_LINE_Y` (y = 80 of the 600-unit world). The rule is in `engine.ts` (#3694) and is built so that it always fires, however fast the player taps. Every count is in fixed physics steps (`FIXED_STEP_MS`, 60 per second), never frames or wall-clock time, so it runs equivalently at 60 Hz and 120 Hz (same simulated time to within one frame; the per-step restructure keeps 60 Hz behaviour equivalent to before, not bit-identical). Frames slower than 20 fps drop their backlog (`step` caps the accumulator at `MAX_SUBSTEPS` steps), so game time then runs slower than the wall clock instead of building a queue.

- **Overflow.** A piece counts when it is _settled_ (its speed at most `OVERFLOW_SETTLED_SPEED` = 8 px per step) and its **top edge** (y − radius) is above the lid. A dropped piece still falling from the spawner never counts, until it first touches another piece or the floor. While at least one piece counts, a counter rises by one each step; a step with none resets it to 0. At `OVERFLOW_TICKS_THRESHOLD` (180 steps, 3 s) the game ends (`gameOver`, reason `overflow`).
- **Merge grace.** A merge involving a piece whose top edge is in the danger band (at or above `OVERFLOW_LINE_Y + DANGER_STACK_MARGIN`, y = 160) resets the counter and pauses it for `OVERFLOW_IGNORE_MERGE_TICKS` (60 steps, 1 s). A merge lower in the bin does nothing. There is no other grace or cooldown. (Before #3694 any merge anywhere reset the counter, so fast play never lost.)
- **Hard stop.** A settled piece whose top edge reaches the top of the board (y ≤ `HARD_STOP_Y` = 0) ends the game at once (reason `hardStop`), so the pile is never drawn off-screen. The last dropped piece gets a short grace: a large piece spawns with its top edge above the canvas (since #3697 droppable pieces spawn on it: on the #3920 ladder the Apple's top edge is at y = 8; a dev-panel piece of tier 6 and up spawns above it) and can touch a pile that reaches just under the lid while still near spawn height, so it must stay settled with its top edge at or above y = 0 for `HARD_STOP_SETTLE_TICKS` (30 steps, 0.5 s) before the hard stop fires. If it really rests there, the game still ends (and the overflow count runs meanwhile).
- **Drop gating.** `canDrop()` is false from a drop until that piece is below the lid (its top edge at or under the line), or merges into a piece that is. The screen ignores taps until then (on top of its 200 ms tap debounce). It cannot deadlock: once the piece lands it counts like any other, so a piece that comes to rest over the lid ends the game through the overflow rule. `drop()` itself is not gated, so the dev panel and the `__cascade_*` test seams can still place pieces.
- **Restore.** A restored game gets the merge grace once (1 s while the pieces settle), then the counter runs as normal. It is one grace per restore: a later restore starts a fresh grace, but does not extend a count already running. The hard stop applies at once to restored pieces that are part of the pile. A saved piece whose top edge is over the lid and that touches no other piece was most likely mid-air (velocities are not saved), so it is treated as falling again (no hard stop or overflow count until its first contact); if exactly one such piece exists it is the last drop for `canDrop()` and the hard-stop settle run.
- **Danger state.** `getState().dangerRatio` is the counter over the threshold (0..1) and `getState().canDrop` the gate. The board draws them (`render/drawFrame.ts`, mappings in `lidVisuals.ts`, colours `CASCADE_LID_PALETTE` in `theme/theme.cascade.ts`): a solid lid line that reddens as the counter runs; a clock ring around the spawner piece (above the lid) that drains clockwise from cyan to red, with a glow from 0.7; a red wash and outline on pieces over the lid while the counter runs; and the spawner piece as a dashed, faded ghost while a drop is not allowed.
- **Regression.** `__tests__/engine.sim.test.ts` runs a bot tapping every 200–250 ms at random x on fixed seeds; it must lose within 120 simulated seconds at 60 and 120 Hz. On #3920's ladder, physics and danger rules it loses in 48–72 s (seeds 1–5: 65.4, 55.3, 60.6, 68.5, 72.0 s at 60 Hz; 57.3, 48.2, 71.8, 51.8, 54.6 s at 120 Hz) and never wins. The longest of the 100-seed suite is 84.9 s at 60 Hz and 84.4 s at 120 Hz, so the bound has about 40 % headroom (#3904: 20 %, longest 99.3 / 100.0 s; #3697: 14 %; Matter.js: 1.6x).
- **Danger rules and mashing (#3904).** The lid's countdown (3 s) and merge grace (1 s) are unchanged; the spawner's danger mode changed twice. Shortening the countdown or the grace could not end a mashing game sooner: in the one spam game that outlived the bound on the first #3904 tuning (seed 72 at 60 Hz) the pile first reaches the lid at about 113 s and keeps dipping back under it, so even a 1.5 s countdown with no merge grace left it alive at 120 s. What kept it alive was danger mode's boost of the small tiers (x1.5 for tiers 0-2), which fed a near-lid pile a stream of easy merges; #3904 removed it. Review then found that the penalty on the big tiers rounded and floored at weight 1, which left tier 7 (weight 1) unpenalised; a plain x0.2 on tiers 3-7 fixed that but left the spam bot's longest game at 116.4 s. So tiers 3-6 are scaled by `DANGER_PENALTY` (0.2) and the top tier, the Coconut / Uranus, by its own milder `DANGER_PENALTY_TOP` (0.35, owner decision on PR #3919). Sweep on the 100-seed spam suite, longest game at 60 / 120 Hz (no spam game won in any): x0.2 110.0 / 116.4 s; **x0.35 99.3 / 100.0 s**; x0.5 100.7 / 96.8 s; x0.7 90.5 / 85.0 s. The skilled bot won 50 % at both 0.35 and 0.5 (heuristic 30 % / 29 %). 0.35 keeps the top tier clearly rarer in danger than calm (6.4 % of draws against 8.0 %; at 0.5 it is 7.8 %, a hair under, and at 0.7 it would come more often in danger); `sim.test.ts` asserts it is under 90 % of its calm share. A careful player barely sees danger: the skilled bot drops in danger mode for 9.6 % of its drops, and the lid's countdown runs in 50 of 100 games, all of them lost, 1.3 times a game, 0.7 % of the time; the heuristic bot never reached the danger band in 100 games of 180 s. The win rates moved within sampling error (skilled 52 % before the danger changes, 50 % after; heuristic 26 % to 30 %). The danger ring is unchanged (it shows the same 3 s count). **#3920** kept the same intent on its 0-5 drops: tiers 2-4 x0.2 and the top tier (5, Apple / Neptune) x0.35, unrounded. With the single-capstone win and drops 0-5 the spam bot loses by 84.9 / 84.4 s and never wins (drops 0-6 let it win 3 games of 100, one reason the owner chose 0-5); the top tier comes 8.4 % of draws in danger against 10.2 % calm.

### Physics

The owner chose **"3 lively"** of the three planck.js proposals (#3697; the spike #3696 and the proposals: [`CASCADE_PHYSICS_SPIKE.md`](../research/CASCADE_PHYSICS_SPIKE.md), [`CASCADE_FEEL_PROPOSALS.md`](../research/CASCADE_FEEL_PROPOSALS.md)). It replaced Matter.js, which teleported pieces back out of the walls about 1,900 times a minute and sank resting pieces 2-3 px into each other.

- **Engine:** planck.js (Box2D in JavaScript), pinned at exactly `1.4.2` in `frontend/package.json`; rendered via `@shopify/react-native-skia`. `physicsWorld.ts` owns the planck `World` and converts at its boundary: 50 px per metre (radii 0.23-1.96 m on the #3920 ladder, inside Box2D's 0.1-10 m), velocities in px per fixed step, angles in radians. Pieces are true circles. planck 1.4.2's polygon mass bug (a dynamic polygon's centre of mass; fixed in 1.5) does not apply: Cascade has only dynamic circles and static boxes. 1.4.2 and 1.5.1 played the spike's 600 games bit for bit the same.
- **Tuning (lively, retuned by #3904):** friction 0.12 (pieces, walls and floor; was 0.2), restitution 0.2, today's gravity (2500 px/s²), air friction 0.01 per step as Box2D linear damping, no rolling damping (`PIECE_ANGULAR_DAMPING` = 0) with spin clamped at `MAX_ANGULAR_VELOCITY`, density `(r0 / r)^0.5` (`tiers.ts`). The solver: each fixed step is `PLANCK_STEPS_PER_FIXED_STEP` (2) planck steps of 8.33 ms, each with 10 velocity / 20 position iterations (#3697: one step, 10 / 8), and planck's Baumgarte factor 0.35 (`POSITION_BAUMGARTE`; planck's default 0.2). The grow-in is 16 steps (was 8). All in `constants.ts`; [Overlap](#overlap-3904) has why.
- **Timestep:** fixed 16.67 ms (60 Hz) with up to 3 fixed steps per frame, each two planck steps; every rule runs once per fixed step, so 60 and 120 Hz play the same rules over the same simulated time.
- **Walls:** 200 px thick, grown outward from the bin's inner faces, and 600 px above the canvas, so nothing tunnels out. **No guard rail in normal play**: nothing is teleported. The only safety left catches a piece that escaped the bin outright (its centre a radius past a wall's or the floor's inner face, or a non-finite position): it is put back and stopped, `guardRailFired` (`escaped`) is emitted, and the screen logs it in dev builds. The sim gate requires 0. Bodies are only removed by a merge.
- **Merges:** two same-tier pieces merge when a planck contact begins between them, or when their circles are within `MERGE_GAP_PX` (1.5 px) at the end of a step (Box2D rests pieces a fraction of a pixel apart, which looks like touching). The scan runs per tier over the same-tier pieces only and does not allocate. The merged piece is made at the parents' midpoint, **clamped so its full-size circle is inside the walls and above the floor**, with the parents' mean velocity (capped at `MAX_SPAWN_VELOCITY`) and a `MERGE_POP_IMPULSE` (0.4 px per step) kick upward.
- **Grow-in:** the merged piece starts at its parents' radius and grows to its own over `GROW_IN_STEPS` (16 steps, 267 ms; 8 before #3904, which on the larger top tiers grew faster than the solver could move the neighbours aside); each step keeps the grown circle inside the bin (a shift of at most one grow step, never a velocity change). Box2D resolves the overlap with a neighbour by position, so a neighbour is moved aside, not shot out (`engine.physics.test.ts` checks no neighbour passes 1.5 px per step). The snapshot carries the piece's `scale` (current / full radius), and the renderer draws it at that size. The lid, the merge grace and the drop gate always use the full radius.
- **Chain reach:** a piece still growing in also merges with a same-tier piece its full radius would touch (`CHAIN_REACH_PX` = 2), without waiting to grow into it, so a chain runs one step per link. Since #3920 it is checked where the product is made, in the step it is made (the merge runs the next step), as well as each step it grows: a product made at its parents' size on top of a third piece can be shoved several px by the solver in its first step (up to 12 px seen in the sim), out of reach of the same-tier piece it was made touching, and the chain was lost (about 2-2.5 % of watched chains on #3904's ladder, 1-1.5 % on this one before the fix; see [Sim harness](#sim-harness-sim-2893)).
- **The win:** making the capstone (#3920), see [The win](#the-win-3920).
- **Sleep:** planck's own (a body at rest for 0.5 s sleeps). The post-physics pass (reading positions, the spin clamp, the safety) skips a body already checked while asleep (#2960): planck neither moves nor spins it.
- **Drop nudge.** `drop(tier, x)` moves the piece by a seeded amount in +-0.4 px (`DROP_NUDGE_PX`) before the wall clamp, so a piece dropped at the same x as the one below it does not land dead centre and stack into a column that never topples. The engine owns the RNG (`EngineConfig.seed` / `seed(n)`; the screen seeds it from the clock, `__cascade_setSeed` reseeds it), one draw per drop. `bench.noDropNudge` switches it off for the lid tests, which land a drop on a restored column.
- **Determinism:** no unseeded randomness anywhere in the engine or planck's solver (the drop nudge is a seeded draw), and piece ids count from 1 per engine, so the same frame deltas replay the same board, bit for bit, at 60 and at 120 Hz (each rate against itself; replays, #3858). 60 and 120 Hz are not bit-identical to each other: they play the same fixed steps and match within a frame (the lid tests). Bit-exact replay holds on one JS engine only: planck calls `Math.sin`, `Math.cos` and `Math.atan2`, which can differ in the last bit between Hermes, JavaScriptCore and V8, so a replay on another device, or on a server, of a chaotic pile can diverge. Do not build anything that needs the same board across engines on it.
- **Allocation:** the engine's own step path (the merge scan, grow-in, chain reach, the post-physics pass and `getState(into)`) allocates nothing per piece. planck's internals do allocate: about 0.4 KB per frame with the pile settled and about 2 KB per frame with 90 awake pieces (measured in review of #3863). The device bench (`Step bench` in the dev panel) is the check, not this note.
- **Events:** `merge` (now with the new piece's `id` and its `parentIds`), `score`, `cascadeCombo`, `gameOver`, `win`, and the dev-only `guardRailFired`.

### Overlap (#3904)

The owner judged the overlap between fruit a must-fix ("it looks bad"). The acceptance, measured in the sim on every run of the suite (each bot at 60 and 120 Hz and the capstone runs; 100 seeds): each game's **deepest overlap between touching neighbours, by drawn radius** (calm samples), **at most 3 px median and 5 px p90 over the games**, and the **mean visible gap at least -0.5 px**. All three are hard gates in `sim/suite.ts` (`OVERLAP_MEDIAN_MAX_PX`, `OVERLAP_P90_MAX_PX`, `GAP_MEAN_MIN_PX`). The median and gap gates apply to every run (the PR smoke, the PR gate, the nightly); the p90 gate only to runs of 100 games or more (`OVERLAP_P90_MIN_GAMES`), i.e. the nightly and manual full runs. A p90 over fewer games is too noisy to gate: the skilled bot read 4.85 px over seeds 1-100 and 5.01 px over the PR gate's seeds 1-50 (review round 1 tuning; 4.91 px over seeds 1-100 as shipped). The V1 ladder on #3697's physics measured 9.4 px median and 14.6 px p90 (skilled bot); #3697's 18-63 ladder 4.6 / 6.7. #3920's 11.5-98 ladder on the same tuning: skilled 1.09 / 3.98 px, every other run under 1 px median and 3 px p90, mean visible gap +0.69 to +0.89 px.

The levers, in the owner's order, each measured on 100 skilled games (10 min cap) and 50-100 games of the other bots (exploration runs with the ladder fixed at V1; "win" is the skilled bot's, "deepest overlap" its median / p90 over the games):

| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------- | -------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |

What the numbers say: the overlap was free room. Every lever that made pieces sit apart also made the pile taller, and the skilled bot's losses are all the lid (under #3904's two-capstone win it made its first capstone in nearly every game, then needed about 100 s for the second). So the density exponent, the owner's first lever, cost the most win rate for the least overlap (heavy pieces sinking through light ones is also what packs the pile), and stays at 0.5. Sub-stepping is what removes the overlap of small pieces squeezed under a deep pile (the tail of the distribution); lower friction gives some of the packing back, but below about 0.12 the spam bot starts to outlive the lid or win. The draw inset (1.5 %) is small: 0.35 px between two cherries on #3920's ladder (0.15 px on #3904's), 2.9 px between two capstones, a mean visible gap of +0.6 to +0.8 px.

The rest of a game's deepest overlap is transients: a merged piece appearing at its parents' size on a piece in the valley between them (one or two frames, pushed out by the solver) and a fast drop landing.

**The spam bot and the lid.** Every setting above sat on a knife edge between three of the owner's limits: the skilled win rate (at least 45 %), the overlap gates, and the spam bot (loses within 120 s, never wins). Friction could not settle it (0.14 ended the spam games and cost the win rate and the overlap). The fix was a danger rule instead: see [Danger rules and mashing](#game-over-the-lid). The win rates move by about 5 points from one 100-seed set to the next (sampling error), so 50 % here is "about half".

### Rendering (#2960)

Nothing per frame goes through React state. The screen's RAF loop steps the engine and reads it with `engine.getState(into)`, which writes into one reused state object (the read allocates nothing per frame; planck's step does allocate a little, see Allocation under Physics). `render/frame.ts` packs what the board draws into a `Float64Array`: a header (piece count, `dangerRatio`, `canDrop`, the spawner's tier) and seven numbers per piece (id, tier, x, y, angle, sleeping, and since #3697 scale: a merged piece's size while it grows in). The loop publishes a copy to a Reanimated shared value only when it differs from the last one published. `components/cascade/CascadeBoard.tsx` records a Skia `Picture` from that shared value in `useDerivedValue` (`createPicture`), on the UI thread, with `render/drawFrame.ts`; the Picture is drawn inside a `Group` scaled from world units to the board.

- **What is drawn.** The same primitives, in the same order and with the same paints, as the declarative `PieceRenderer` and the #3694 lid components it replaced: each piece (its three cached art bitmaps at the ladder radius, see [Art (#3698)](#art-3698), or a placeholder circle) with its over-lid wash, then the lid line, then the spawner (piece, waiting ghost, clock ring). Every lid decision comes from `lidVisuals.ts`, whose mappings are worklets. A headless CanvasKit comparison of the old and new renderers on real engine states (light and dark, sprites and placeholders, calm, waiting, danger 0.5 and 0.9) was pixel-identical. That comparison ran headless, on one thread (CanvasKit, with the Picture recorded and drawn on the JS thread); it has not been verified on a device, where the Picture is recorded on the UI thread. Checking the board on iOS and Android is an owner follow-up (the Maestro screenshots of #3705, or a device session). `render/__tests__/worklets.test.ts` runs the real worklets Babel plugin to check that every function the UI-thread recording reaches is a worklet, which the Jest Reanimated stub cannot.
- **Sleeping pieces** are drawn where they fell asleep (the #1746 freeze, kept in the packer). A settled board therefore packs the same frame every time and publishes nothing.
- **Score.** `scoreStore.ts` holds the live score; `ScoreDisplay` subscribes with `useSyncExternalStore` and re-renders only when the integer changes. `ScoreDisplay`, `ThemeSelector`, `NextFruitPreview` and `CascadeBoard` are `React.memo`; the screen's handlers and styles are hoisted.
- **Events.** Merges and game over still reach the screen through `onMergeRef` / `onGameOverRef`; a merge burst (`components/cascade/MergeBurst.tsx`) is the one React update a merge makes, besides the score (none for the winning merge, #3699).
- **The win celebration (#3699)** is drawn into the same Picture from a second shared value, its clock: see [The celebration and the win card](#the-celebration-and-the-win-card-3699).
- **Measured.** `CascadeScreen.frames.test.tsx` counts React commits with a `Profiler`: 60 frames commit 60 times before #2960 (settled or moving) and 0 times after; a score change commits once. The dev panel's _Frame_ line (Star Swarm's `render/frameStats.ts` ring buffer, dev builds only) shows frame time and commits per second on a device; commits are counted by a `Profiler` around the screen, so a commit of `ScoreDisplay` alone counts. Numbers are in [PERFORMANCE.md](../PERFORMANCE.md#cascade-renderer-2960).
- **Web (CanvasKit, #2960 part b).** On web the same Skia code draws the board, through Skia's web runtime: CanvasKit, Skia compiled to WebAssembly. Owner decision 2026-11-11 on #2960: one drawing path on every platform, so web demos (the only demos a cloud session can record itself) show what the phones show; a separate Canvas 2D board like Mahjong's would draw everything twice and drift. How it loads:
  - `lazyScreens.ts` routes Cascade to `screens/CascadeRoute`. On iOS and Android `CascadeRoute.tsx` is the screen itself. On web Metro picks `CascadeRoute.web.tsx`, which shows a loading state, calls `loadCascadeScreen.web.ts` (CanvasKit first, then `import("./CascadeScreen")` in its own chunk) and renders the screen. The order matters: `react-native-skia`'s web module builds its `Skia` API from `global.CanvasKit` when it is first evaluated, so nothing that imports Skia may be evaluated earlier. This is the pattern of Skia's `WithSkiaWeb`, written out so a failure can be retried.
  - `game/cascade/render/loadCanvasKit.ts` mirrors Skia's `LoadSkiaWeb`, with two changes: the `.wasm` comes from Metro's asset pipeline (`require` of `canvaskit-wasm/bin/full/canvaskit.wasm`; `.wasm` is in `metro.config.js` `assetExts`), so the web export writes it to `dist/assets/node_modules/canvaskit-wasm/bin/full/canvaskit.<hash>.wasm` and it is served from our own origin, never a CDN; and a failed load is forgotten, so Retry tries again (`LoadSkiaWeb` keeps a rejected promise). `canvaskit-wasm` is a direct dependency pinned to the version `@shopify/react-native-skia` depends on (0.41.0 for Skia 2.14.0); `render/__tests__/canvasKitWeb.test.ts` fails if they drift, or if a non-web file imports the loader.
  - A load that fails or takes longer than 60 s (`CANVASKIT_LOAD_TIMEOUT_MS`) shows a readable error with Try again inside the game shell (`cascade-web-error`), never an endless spinner (#2539). Only the first failure in a page session reports a Sentry event (`subsystem: cascade.canvaskit`); later ones leave a breadcrumb. Retry after a timeout starts a fresh download. A browser that blocks WebAssembly under its Content-Security-Policy gets the same error: the deployed dev site's CSP allows `'wasm-unsafe-eval'` for this (`render.yaml`; prod gets it when Cascade ships there, #3703).
  - Nothing else loads CanvasKit: the lobby and the other web games never request the `.wasm`, and Mahjong and Star Swarm keep their Canvas 2D `GameCanvas.web.tsx` (the Playwright spec checks both). The iOS and Android bundles hold neither the loader nor the `.wasm` (checked on an Android export, #2960 part b). Download cost: [PERFORMANCE.md](../PERFORMANCE.md#cascade-on-web-canvaskit-2960).
  - Board accessibility: the board's role and label sit on a `View` around the Skia `Canvas`, because Skia's web `Canvas` drops accessibility props.
  - Skia features the renderer uses, checked on web (Chromium, WebGL): `createPicture` / `Picture` inside a scaled `Group`, `drawCircle`, `drawLine`, `drawImageRectOptions` with the cached piece bitmaps (`pieceSprites`), `Path.MakeFromSVGString` + `drawPath`, `PathEffect.MakeDash` (the waiting ghost) and `MaskFilter.MakeBlur` (the clock ring's glow). The art (#3698) uses gradients, but only when it rasterises a bitmap, on the JS thread (CanvasKit's CPU surface on web); the per-frame Picture draws bitmaps only. Motion (#3701) adds `drawRect`, stroked `drawPath` of cached paths, `PathEffect.MakeDash` (cached per offset) and transforms only; checked on web (Playwright recording on the PR), a phone check is part of its device demo. Not yet compared side by side with a phone screenshot (the Maestro screenshots of #3705).
  - E2E: `e2e/tests/cascade-smoke.spec.ts` (Playwright project `cascade`).

### Art (#3698)

All 22 pieces are drawn in code (and three retired ones are kept as recipes, see [Retired pieces](#retired-pieces-3920)); Cascade has no image files. The owner chose the style on 2026-11-14 from three proposals (issue #3698): **kawaii glossy sticker**. The art is original to the project (see [ASSETS.md](../ASSETS.md)).

- **Style.** A glossy gradient body, a darker rim just inside the edge, outlined features, two specular dots and a face. Fruits have open eyes and a smile; cosmos pieces have closed eyes and blush, so pieces that share a hue (Pluto, Mercury; Neptune, Uranus) also differ by marks. #3920 made the Apple a bright glossy green (`CASCADE_ART.fruits.apple`), so it no longer reads as the Cherry; it stays apart from the Lemon (yellow) and the Watermelon (dark, striped). Only paths, linear and radial gradients, circles and round-capped strokes are used: no `BlurMask`, no blur, no bitmaps, no shader per frame. Colours are tokens in `CASCADE_ART` (`src/theme/theme.cascade.ts`), so the colour gate sees no literals in the art code.
- **Modules** (`frontend/src/game/cascade/art/`).
  - `pen.ts`: how a piece is painted (the gradients, outline, specular dots, face) and the three layers. A piece says what is on it, in unit space: centre (0, 0), collider radius 1, y down.
  - `fruits.ts`: the eleven Fruits, the Dragonfruit (tier 7, take B) included, and the retired Peach and red Apple.
  - `cosmos.ts`: the eleven Cosmos pieces, and the retired Moon.
  - `sprites.ts`: the caches, and `piecePicture` for icons.
  - `bin.ts`: the bin (see Bin below).
  - Adding or changing a piece edits `fruits.ts` or `cosmos.ts` only; run `UPDATE_RASTER_REFS=1 npx jest art.test` to rewrite the reference sheet and review the image diff.
- **Drawn radius.** The body is clipped to the unit circle and drawn at `tierDrawnRadius(tier)` from `tiers.ts`: the ladder radius less the 1.5 % draw inset (#3904). There is no padded bitmap and no other scale factor between the art and the physics circle. `art.test.ts` checks that the body fills the drawn circle to its edge and that nothing outside it is drawn except the declared overhangs. On the #3920 ladder the Cherry is drawn 22.7 px across in world units (about 22 pt on a 390 pt phone, 66 bitmap pixels at 3x; 9.6 px on #3904's). The bitmap cache copes with the 11.5-98 range as it is: one bitmap pixel per screen pixel at each tier's drawn radius, `pxPerUnit` unchanged.
- **Caching: three layers, light upright, faces roll.** Each tier is three CPU bitmaps (`pieceSprites(setId, pxPerUnit)`): `body` (back overhangs and the clipped body; rotates with the piece), `light` (the rim shade, gloss and specular dots; upright, so the highlight stays top-left however the piece rolls) and `top` (the face and the front overhangs; rotates). The face rolls with the body, as a sticker on it would (owner, 2026-11-14); an upright face over a rolling body slides across features such as the coconut's eyes. The board draws each piece as three `drawImageRect`s. The bitmap scale is `pxPerUnit` = board scale x device pixel ratio, so a piece is one bitmap pixel per screen pixel at its full radius and is never scaled up (only down, for the grow-in and the spawner's copy). Cache keys are the set and `pxPerUnit` rounded up to 0.05 (so a pixel-or-two resize reuses the set); the last 3 are kept and an evicted key's bitmaps are disposed (`MAX_SPRITE_SETS`). Icons (next-piece preview, theme selector, dev panel, home tile) use `piecePicture(set, tier, size)`: one upright vector `SkPicture` per set, tier and size, sharp at any pixel ratio. A tier that cannot be rasterised stays `null` and the board draws the placeholder circle.
- **Sizes.** Art code: 17.4 KB minified (7.2 KB gzipped) for both sets, under the 20 KB budget. Bitmap memory per set: 5.3 to 6.9 MB on #3697's ladder (the old sprites were about 23 MB); the #3904 ladder's areas sum about 26 % larger (sum of r² 22,250 against 18,212), so about 6.7 to 8.7 MB, not re-measured on a device. Building a set takes 75 to 91 ms, on the JS thread when the screen opens and when the set switches. Recording a 60-piece frame takes 0.54 ms with the JIT and 0.75 ms jitless (headless CanvasKit; phones are the owner's Manual QA).
- **Overhangs.** Only the pieces below draw outside the collider circle. Values are how far the ink reaches past the circle in radii, measured with the outline strokes included; the owner approved these measured values on 2026-11-14 (they are slightly more than the proposal's, which left the strokes out). `art/__tests__/art.test.ts` measures every piece and pins each value to within 0.006 r, and each piece's `box` (the bitmap bounds) to within 0.03 r of its ink.

### Piece Queue & Spawn Selection (`pieceQueue.ts`, `spawnSelector.ts`)

- Queue holds `{ current, next }` tiers; `advanceQueue()` shifts forward and generates a new `next`
- Tier selection uses a weighted random algorithm:
  1. Base weights over tiers 0-5 (`2 3 3 3 2 1`, #3920): a drop carries about 7.7 Cherries' worth of material, so the win (one capstone, 1024 Cherries) is about 133 perfect drops away (#3904's V1: tiers 0-7 weighted `2 3 3 3 3 3 2 1`, 22.2 Cherries a drop, two capstones to win; #3697: 0-5 weighted `2 3 3 3 3 2`)
  2. Drought boost: tiers absent from the last 10 drops gain +3 weight
  3. Danger mode: when the stack is near the overflow line, tiers 2-4 are scaled to 20% of their weight and the top tier (5) to 35% (#3920; on #3904's ladder tiers 3-6 and 7, the same intent; fractional; #3697 rounded and kept at least weight 1, which left the weight-1 tier 7 unpenalised; the milder top-tier factor is the owner's PR #3919 decision, see [Danger rules and mashing](#game-over-the-lid)). (#3697 also multiplied tiers 0-2 by 1.5; #3904 removed that, see [Danger rules and mashing](#game-over-the-lid).)
  4. Streak hard-ban: if the last 4 picks are identical, that tier is banned for one turn
- RNG is injectable for deterministic testing and seeded replay

### Combo Detection (`engine.ts`)

A `cascadeCombo` event is emitted when ≥3 merges occur within `COMBO_WINDOW_TICKS` (120 ticks, ~2 s). The combo counter resets on each drop. The window is counted in fixed physics steps, so it is 2 s of simulated time at any frame rate (it used to be counted per frame: 2 s at 60 Hz, 1 s at 120 Hz).

### Sim harness (`sim/`, #2893)

A seeded, headless sim in `frontend/src/game/cascade/sim/` plays the real `CascadeEngine`, spawn selector and queue (`createPieceQueue` / `advanceQueue`, fed by the shared seeded RNG) the way `CascadeScreen` does: taps are gated by `canDrop()`, `isInDanger` comes from the pile height. It is the measuring tool for the physics spike (#3696), the physics story (#3697), the lid (#3694) and the tier ladder (#3695). Same seed, bot, frame rate and engine give the same metrics (step cost aside).

- **Bots** (`bots.ts`): `heuristic` (waits for a calm board, drops where it lands on a same-tier piece, else lowest), `random` (600-1200 ms, random x), `spam` (200-250 ms, random x; the lid test's bot), `skilled` (#3697: the heuristic's timing, but it keeps the pile graded, big pieces left and small right, sets up merges next to a piece one tier away and avoids burying small pieces; the capstone runs' bot). The sim test compares the heuristic and random bots by merges per drop, not by score: on the #3904 ladder the random bot survives the 180 s cap in almost every game and drops about a quarter more pieces (it does not wait for a calm board), so its total score is higher although it merges less per drop (0.58 against 0.74).
- **Frame rates:** every run is done at 60 and 120 Hz frame deltas.
- **Metrics** (`runGame.ts`, `suite.ts`): piece frequency per tier; drought (longest run of drops without a tier); game length (simulated s), score, score by tier, top-tier reached rate; win rate and time to win (the engine's `win` event, #3697; making the capstone since #3920), the time to the win (p25 / median / p75 of the games that won) and to the tier below the capstone (`preCapstone.timeS.median`, tier 9 since #3920; `tier10` before); every run ends at the win, since play ends there (#3920; only the capstone runs did before); physics feel: packing density, visible gap between touching neighbours (drawn radius), settle time after a drop and after a 3+ merge chain, jitter of settled pieces (px/s), guard-rail events per game (`guardRailFired`, counted, never acted on: #1716); wall penetration (deepest, and pieces more than 1 px in per minute, read every frame at each piece's current size); chain merge latency (a merge product touching a same-tier piece at full size when made, timed until it merges again, from the merge events' `id` / `parentIds`; one not merged within 1 s counts as a miss at 1000 ms; a watch still open at the win is dropped, since nothing merges after it); step cost (wall clock, printed but not in the baseline). Hard gates (not bands): the spam bot always reaches game over inside 120 simulated seconds and never wins (#3904), no guard rail fires, chain-merge latency p90 is under 150 ms, each run's deepest overlap is at most 3 px median over its games (and at most 5 px p90 in runs of 100 games) with a mean visible gap of at least -0.5 px (#3904, see [Overlap](#overlap-3904)), and the mean step with 20+ pieces is under 16 ms.
- **1 px-contact spawn helper** (`contactSpawn.ts`): `contactPair(tier, x)` places two same-tier pieces overlapped by 1 px, for merge tests (planck's circles are exact; on Matter it added a polygon-facet allowance). Deep overlaps can separate before `collisionStart` fires and the merge silently fails (#1296, #1302, #1418).
- **Comparing a candidate** (#3696): `runGame` / `runSuite` take a `SimConfig`: `createEngine` (a factory for any engine with the `SimEngine` surface) and `constants` (overrides for what the sim and its metrics read: world size, lid y, physics and drawn radius per tier, score per tier, droppable tiers). The real engine reads its tuning from `constants.ts` at import time, so a candidate engine brings its own numbers and the same numbers go in `constants`.
- **Run it:** `cd frontend && npm run sim:cascade` (smoke, 3 seeds, about 30 s) or `npm run sim:cascade -- --full` (100 seeds x 3 bots x 60/120 Hz plus the capstone runs, 100 skilled games at 60 Hz of up to 10 simulated minutes each that stop at the win; about 30 min since #3904 (12 before; about 18 min in this container on #3920's ladder); `--no-capstone` skips them). `--gate` checks `sim/baseline.json` and exits 1 on a violation; `--update-baseline` rewrites the baseline for that suite (then `npx prettier --write` the file); `--md PATH` / `--json PATH` write the table / raw result.
- **Baseline and bands:** `sim/baseline.json` holds today's deterministic metrics with a band per metric (relative plus an absolute floor per unit; rates never tighter than one game of n). Bands are drift guards around today's physics, not targets; #3695 / #3697 re-baselined. The smoke suite is a coarse guard: it bands only game length, score and guard-rail rate with wide tolerances (checked with the seed set shifted), and the lid and step budget are hard gates; per-tier rates, shares, droughts and settle are banded in the full suite only, which assumes 100 seeds (a run with another seed count prints the table and skips the bands).
- **Settle time is survivor-biased.** A drop that arrives before the board calms cuts that episode off, and it never reaches the mean. The table prints the cut-off fraction next to it (`settle episodes cut off by the next drop`): about 0.5 for the heuristic and random bots, 0.99 for spam. So the **spam bot's settle numbers are not comparable** (they come from a few unusual episodes) and are never banded; the heuristic and random settle means are banded only when 20 or more episodes finished. Compare settle between engines for the same bot and read it with its cut-off fraction. Speed thresholds (calm 18 px/s, settled 30 px/s) are per second, so 60 and 120 Hz compare.
- **CI:** the smoke runs in every PR's Jest run (`sim/__tests__/sim.test.ts`, about 8 s); the long run is `.github/workflows/cascade-sim-gate.yml` (nightly, on demand, and on PRs that touch the engine, its constants, the queue or the sim). PR runs play 50 capstone seeds (about 7 min before #3904, about 2.4x since) instead of 100, with the capstone bands widened by sqrt(100 / 50) (`--capstone-seeds`, `baselineForCapstoneSeeds`); nightly and manual runs play 100.

## Scoring (Persistence)

- **Metric and direction:** `final_score`, higher is better, labelled `score` (`board` in `backend/cascade/module.py`; `BOARDS.cascade` in `frontend/src/api/vocab.ts`). Since #3700 it is the **merge score plus a win's time bonus**, computed by the server. The merge score is the points at game over: for each merge the engine (`engine.ts`) adds the new piece's `score` from `tiers.ts`.
- **Time bonus (#3700, owner decision 2026-11-16, "Balanced").** `time_bonus = round(20000 × max(0, 1 − t / 360000))` for a win, 0 for a loss, where `t` is the completion's `duration_ms` (active play, see Duration). `B` = 20,000, `T` = 6 min. Rounding is half up in integer arithmetic on both sides, so the app and the server agree to the point.
  - **Floor:** `MIN_WIN_MS` = 20 s. A win under it, or with no `duration_ms`, gets a bonus of 0. It is stored and ranks on its merge score; it is not rejected (a 400 dead-letters the game). The fastest possible win is about 22-25 s (about 110 drops at the 200 ms drop cooldown); the fastest sim win took 36.9 s.
  - **Why these numbers:** sim of about 1,600 games on #3924 (#3700 comments). Merge score grows with game length at about 35 points/s, so the bonus slope (`B / T`, 56 points/s) has to beat it or slower wins outrank faster ones. With these numbers every bot win outranks every bot loss (median win total 16,402; best loss 12,094), halving the median win time is worth +5,872, and merge score still separates wins of the same pace. Revisit `T` against real `duration_ms` after launch.
  - **One source of truth:** `backend/cascade/time_bonus.py` (`BONUS_MAX`, `PAR_TIME_MS`, `MIN_WIN_MS` and the version `BONUS_RULE` = 1). The server recomputes the bonus from the completion's `outcome` and `duration_ms` columns and ignores the app's copy. The app mirrors it for display only (`frontend/src/game/cascade/timeBonus.ts`). Both are checked against `backend/tests/fixtures/cascade_time_bonus.json` (`backend/tests/test_cascade_result.py`, `frontend/src/game/cascade/__tests__/timeBonus.test.ts`). A retune bumps `BONUS_RULE`, so rows scored under the old rule can be told apart without a migration.
  - **Old rows and old builds:** a completion without `merge_score` (a build before #3700) keeps the score it sent, with no bonus, and ranks by it. Abandons keep the score sent and never rank.
  - **Known gap (own story, before the leaderboard launch):** the play clock uses `Date.now()`, so setting the device clock back mid-game shortens `t` and raises the bonus (about 3,300 points per minute). The floor catches a big jump back, not a few minutes. A monotonic clock fixes it in the shared `playClock.ts`.
- **Tie-break:** none declared (the research on #3700 dropped a `duration_ms` tie-break: boards don't support one without new board code and an index). Equal scores go to the entry the server received first (`received_at`), the last tie-break on every board.
- **Partitions:** none: one board.
- **Recorded, not partitioned:** the result block (see below). The fruit set is in the `game_started` event (`fruit_set`, `theme`), not in metadata.

### The result block (#3700)

Built by `cascadeResultBlock` (`frontend/src/game/cascade/resultBlock.ts`) for the completion, the screen's abandons and the unmount snapshot, validated by `CascadeResult` and merged into `games.metadata`.

| Key                                                   | Written by | Notes                                                                                                                                                     |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | ---------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `merge_score`                                         | app        | The merge points (the engine's score).                                                                                                                    |
| `max_tier`                                            | app        | The highest tier on the board this game, 0-10; 10 on a win. For a goal like "make a tier-9 piece" (#3703).                                                |
| `won`                                                 | server     | `outcome == "win"`. The Daily Challenge goals read it (#3703). The app sends its own copy; the server's wins.                                             |
| `outcome`                                             | server     | `win` / `loss` from the completion's outcome; left out otherwise (the `games.outcome` column has it).                                                     |
| `time_bonus`                                          | server     | Recomputed from the completion's `outcome` and `duration_ms`; the app's copy is display-only and replaced. Set only when the block carries `merge_score`. |
| `bonus_rule`                                          | server     | The rule version (1). Set with `time_bonus`.                                                                                                              |
| `final_score`                                         | server     | `merge_score + time_bonus` on a finished game (an abandon keeps the sent one). Equal to the `final_score` column.                                         |
| `duration_ms`, `theme`, `total_drops`, `total_merges` | app        | As before #3700. The `duration_ms` column is the authority.                                                                                               |

- `capstone` (the AC's name for the set id) is not sent: it duplicates `theme`.
- **Cost and volume (CLAUDE.md priorities):** about 100 bytes of JSON more per finished game (`merge_score`, `time_bonus`, `bonus_rule`, `max_tier`, `won`; `resultBlock.test.ts` keeps it under 120), on the game's existing row: no new rows, no per-event data, nothing uploaded during play (the block goes once at game end through `SyncWorker`). At 1,000 games a day that is about 100 KB a day, bounded by games played; Cascade is premium-only, so the volume tracks revenue. No dial or kill switch is needed for its size; it rides the existing result envelope.
- **Max value:** none (#2519 decision 14). Any integer up to 2³¹−1 is accepted.
- **Outcomes:** `has_winner = True` since #3699. Making the capstone records `win`; the lid's game over records `loss`. Builds before #3699 sent `completed` for both, which stays a finish with no winner. Restart during play records `abandoned` (`handleRestart`), and so does switching the fruit set mid-game (the fruit-set effect in `CascadeScreen.tsx` calls `endInstrumentedSession("abandoned")` and starts a new game). Leaving the screen records `abandoned` too (`useGameSync`'s unmount abandon), once a piece has been dropped. The Restart and fruit-set abandons carry the score so far in `final_score`, but abandoned rows never rank or count toward "best".
- **Duration:** Cascade's own `duration_ms` (`playedMs` in `CascadeScreen.tsx`): time since the game's first piece went in (a drop, or a test or dev placement; #3700, `startClockOnMove`; before it, since the session opened), less the time the player was away. A new game (mount, restart, fruit-set switch) starts with the clock not started, so time looking at an empty board doesn't count against the time bonus. The clock stops for good at the win or the lid, so the session and the card read the same time. Being away is another screen covering the board (navigation `blur`, #2743) or the app in the background (`AppState`, #2750): `usePauseWhileAway` stops the physics loop and the clock, a `PlayClock` (`startedAt` / `accumulatedMs`, `frontend/src/game/_shared/playClock.ts`) kept in a ref. Each animation frame carries the loop's generation, so a frame the OS delivers late after a trip to the background can't start a second loop. Because the value is > 0 it wins over `useGameSync`'s active-play window. The saved board carries `playedMs` (`SavedState` in `frontend/src/game/cascade/storage.ts`), and the pause saves the board, so a game continued after an app kill keeps the play before it and doesn't count the time the app was closed. A save from a build before #2750 has no `playedMs`: its earlier play is unknown, so the game counts from the relaunch.
- **How it reaches the server:** the `useGameSync("cascade")` session row. `SyncWorker` sends `POST /games` after the first drop (`markStarted`) and `PATCH /games/{id}/complete` at game over. If the player has a display name (`PUT /players/me`), the row ranks with no further step. The board shows each named player's best game once. Shared rules: [Leaderboard routes](../GAME-CONTRACT.md#leaderboard-routes-2618).
- **Where the player sees it:** the result card shows the rank through `lookupGameRank` (`GET /games/{id}/rank`), or asks once for a display name. The card's "View leaderboard" link and the ⋯ menu open the Leaderboard screen (#2633). Stats (#2635) are in the ⋯ menu: the shared Stats screen shows Cascade's wins, losses, win rate and win streaks (`has_winner`) and Best (the highest score, bonus included). It has no "fastest win" tile: that needs a new shared stats aggregate (#3700 left it as a follow-up); the time of each win is in `duration_ms` and on the win card. Store builds hide Cascade (`HIDDEN_GAMES`, `frontend/src/entitlements/gameVisibility.ts`), so there it has no leaderboard or stats entry point.

## Accessibility

Cascade uses a Skia canvas for rendering. The native accessibility tree does not include canvas content. Requirements:

- Current score must be displayed in a native `Text` element outside the canvas
- Game-over state must be announced via an accessible modal or live region
- Canvas element marked `accessible={false}`

See [`docs/ACCESSIBILITY.md §4`](../ACCESSIBILITY.md#4-screen-readers) for the full canvas accessibility contract.
