# Cascade decision log and implementation reference

> **Not the rules.** The rules are in the canonical specification, [`cascade.md`](cascade.md). This file is the working detail behind them: the numbers, the module map, the renderer, the art, the sim, and the owner decisions and measurements that produced the rules. Where a parenthesis compares a rule with an earlier design, it is history.

## Piece Progression

Pieces evolve through **12 fixed tiers** (0-11, #3960; 11 since #3920, 12 before that). The lowest-tier pieces are droppable (tiers 0-5); higher tiers are only reachable via merging. Two pieces of tier n (n < 11) merge into tier n + 1; two tier-10 pieces make the **capstone** (tier 11, Pumpkin / Milky Way), which is never dropped (`MAX_TIER` = 11). **Making the capstone is the win** (#3920): see [The win](#the-win-3920). #3960 lengthened the ladder by one tier to make the game a little harder: the Moon is back at tier 1 of Cosmos, Fruits gain a Lime and swap Blueberry for Lychee, Grape for Plum and Coconut for Pomegranate (see [retired pieces](#retired-pieces-3920); the Fruits art for the four new fruits was drawn by #3964, see [Art](#art-3698)).

**One table.** `frontend/src/game/cascade/tiers.ts` is the only place a tier's radius, score, density, droppable flag and name keys are defined. The engine (`pieceDefs.ts`), the board renderer, the spawner and its lid ring, the next-piece preview, the theme selector, the dev panel and the i18n keys all read it. `theme/fruitSets.ts` keeps only per-set names, colours and sprite references (no emoji: the owner removed the stand-ins in #3964). `tiers.test.ts` fails if any other module defines a per-tier radius, score or density.

- **Radii** (#3920, owner decision after playing #3904's V1, which felt too small): one constant ratio for the whole ladder, `radius(n) = 11 * k^n` with k = (98 / 11)^(1/11) = 1.21997 since #3967 (R0 = 11, owner decision #3978; #3960 had 11.5 and k = 1.21505, the 11-tier ladder 1.23895), **unrounded**, so every tier-to-tier ratio is exactly k and the capstone is exactly 98. The Cherry is about 6.1 % of the bin's 360 px inner width across (6.4 % at 11.5, the share in the owner's reference game), about 21 pt on a 390 pt phone (the board is about 0.97 pt per world unit); the Pumpkin stays about half the bin. (#3904's V1 was `5 * (98 / 5)^(n / 11)` on 12 tiers; #3697's ladder was `round(18 * (56 / 18)^(n / 10))`, 18 to 56, with the capstone at 63 off the curve.) (#3960 kept both ends, 11.5 and 98, and put one more tier between them, so every tier is a little smaller than the same tier number was on the 11-tier ladder; #3967 then made the Cherry 11, which took the skilled bot from about 40 % to 46 %, so the capstone's mass is 26.6 times a cherry, 24.9 before.) Why a single capstone, and why #3920 chose 11 tiers: on 12 tiers the bigger cherry could not be won (0 of 100 skilled games; two capstones take 4,096 cherries' worth of material, each now about 5 times the area), and two-capstone wins on 10 or 11 tiers stayed at 4-24 %; issue #3920 has the measured variants.
- **Drawn radius** is 1.5 % inside the collider (`DRAW_INSET`, `tierDrawnRadius`, #3904): the last of the owner's overlap levers, see [Overlap](#overlap-3904). The board, the art bitmaps and the spawner draw at it; physics, the lid, spawning and merges use the radius.
- **Droppable tiers** are 0-5 (#3920). The largest (Orange / Earth, r = 29.7) spawns with its top edge at y = 15.5, on the canvas.
- **Scores** double per tier: a merge into tier n scores `2^n`; the winning merge into the capstone scores its normal 2048, with no extra (#3920, #3960). The merge-score values are final (owner, #2823); the time bonus (#3700, see [Scoring (Persistence)](#scoring-persistence)) was set against them, so a win dominates a long losing run. There is no jackpot bonus.
- **Density** is planck.js's fixture density, `(r0 / r)^0.5` (#3697): mass grows as r^1.5 instead of r^2. On this ladder the capstone is 26.6 times a cherry (24.9 at R0 = 11.5) (87 on #3904's, 6.5 on #3697's). #3904 tried flattening it (exponents 0.75 to 1.37, down to today's 6.5 : 1) as the owner's first overlap lever; see [Overlap](#overlap-3904) for why it stayed. Only the ratios matter.
- **Art.** All 24 pieces (12 fruits, 12 cosmos) are drawn in code, in `game/cascade/art/`; see [Art (#3698)](#art-3698). A tier whose art cannot be rasterised is drawn as a plain circle in its placeholder colour.
- **Names** are `cascade:pieces.<set>.<key>` in every locale (24 keys; #3920 removed `fruits.peach` and `cosmos.moon`, #3960 added `fruits.lychee`, `fruits.lime`, `fruits.plum`, `fruits.pomegranate` and `cosmos.moon`; #3965 translated the lychee, lime, plum, pomegranate and moon names in all 12 non-English locales and removed the unused `fruits.blueberry`, `fruits.grape` and `fruits.coconut` keys; the other names still carry the English copy in locales, tracked in `scripts/i18n-english-copies.json`).

## The win (#3920)

- **Rule.** Making the capstone is the win: the merge of two tier-10 pieces (Watermelon / Sun) into the capstone (tier 11, Pumpkin / Milky Way), by any merge path (contact, the 1.5 px merge-gap scan, chain reach). It is an ordinary merge (the `merge` event and its `score`, the capstone's 2048, no extra), then `{ type: "win", x, y, id }`: where the capstone was made, and its id. It replaced #3904's rule (two capstones touching, both burst, 2 x 2048), which #3920 removed: two capstones never merge and are not a win (only the dev panel or a restore can put two on the board).
- **The end of play (engine gate).** From the step of the win, `canDrop()` is false and `drop()` does nothing (so the screen's taps stop, and the spawner shows its waiting ghost), no other merge runs (one pending in the same step included) and the lid rule is off (no `gameOver` follows a win), and the score is final. The engine itself keeps stepping the physics when asked, so the capstone grows in. `hasWon()` reads it; `restore()` starts a new game. The engine owns the gate so the screen, the test seams and the sim all see the same end; the screen keeps its frame loop running after the `win` event until no piece is growing in (the capstone is made at its parents' size and grows in over `GROW_IN_STEPS`, about 0.27 s), plus 100 ms to settle (`game/cascade/winTail.ts`, at most 240 frames), then stops it, so the board stays on a full-size capstone (a background trip in that window pauses the tail and the resume finishes it, never a new loop); at the win it completes the session (outcome `win`, #3699), keeps the best and clears the saved game, saving nothing more (pause, background or drop), so a relaunch starts a new game and a won game is never restored. The test hook state carries `won`.
- **Why the engine and not the screen.** One gate, the one the screen already reads for taps (`canDrop`).

Owner pick (2026-11-15, from three proposals on #3699): **A "Spotlight", with C's cheering face**. Everything is drawn in the board's Skia Picture on the UI thread; nothing per frame goes through React or the JS loop.

| 400-850          | The capstone rises to the middle of the bin under the lid, growing to 1.32x and turning upright (easeOutBack)                                                              |

- **Reduce Motion (the calm version).** No rise, rotation, pulse, rays, swirl or twinkles. The dim, glow, ring and cheering face fade in where the capstone lies (400-600 ms), and the card comes at 1.6 s. Reduce Motion switched mid-celebration carries on at the same time on the other timeline; if that one is already over, the card comes at once.
- **No merge burst** for the winning merge: the celebration replaces it.
- **Modules.** The timeline is `game/cascade/celebration.ts` (pure numbers, worklets, unit-tested). `render/drawCelebration.ts` draws it over the board; `drawFrame`'s `hideTier` leaves the capstone out underneath, so it is drawn above the dim. `render/celebrationStyle.ts` builds once (cached by set, theme and pixel scale), ahead of the win (see Pre-warm), everything it draws with: the paths, gradients, twinkle and star tables, and the capstone rasterised again at 1.4x the board's pixel scale (`art/sprites.ts`'s `capstoneSprite`, with a fourth layer, `cheer`, from `art/pen.ts`), so the 1.32x capstone (1.37x at a pulse peak) is drawn down from a bitmap, never up (about 4 x 275² px at a 3x phone; the last two are kept). Colours are `CASCADE_CELEBRATION` in `theme/theme.cascade.ts`: the art's colours and the theme's background and celebration tokens (`gamePalettes.test.ts`).
- **Pre-warm (iOS demo on #3924).** Built in the win's render, the style held the JS thread while the UI-thread clock ran on: the board showed the plain capstone for about 1.3 s, then the finished spotlight, its dim and rise never seen. Now the screen flags the board once it holds a tier-10 piece (a merge into tier 9, a restored save, a set-up or a spawn), and `components/cascade/useCelebrationStyle.ts` builds the style in idle time (`requestIdleCallback`, 1.5 s deadline), never on the frame or gesture path; only games one merge from the win pay for it. A win before that has run builds it at once, after the win's commit. Measured on web (Chromium, CanvasKit): 10 ms at 1x, 50 ms at 3x; a `__DEV__` (and test-hook build) log prints each build's time on a device.
- **Clock.** `CascadeCelebration` (rendered by `GameResultModal` as its `celebration` at the win) steps a shared value on the UI thread, one frame at a time (`useFrameCallback`, `stepCelebrationClock`), each frame moving it at most `MAX_FRAME_STEP_MS` (two 60 Hz frames). It starts only when the board is ready to draw the celebration (`onCelebrationReady`: the game is won and the style is built, or cannot be, with no Skia), at the time since the win but never past the 400 ms grow-in (`celebrationStartMs`), so the card comes about 2.1 s after the celebration can be seen. The frame that reaches the card's time calls `done`; a JS timer (the card's time plus `FALLBACK_SLACK_MS`, 1 s) calls it if frames never get there. It is no Reanimated animation, so the OS Reduce Motion setting cannot shorten it; the calm visuals come from `reduceMotion`. `CascadeBoard`'s `useDerivedValue` closes over the clock, so Reanimated re-runs it (and re-records the Picture) on every step (`worklets.test.ts` checks the closure). `GameResultModal` mounts the card only after `done`, so the card never covers the animation (#2201); its 4 s safety net (`CELEBRATION_MAX_MS`, from the win) still shows the card if the board never becomes ready.
- **Why stepped, and the warm-up (second iOS demo on #3924).** In the iOS recording the screen redraws at about 60 fps once the celebration shows, so the Picture does follow the clock; but nothing is drawn for about 0.44 s before the first celebration frame, and that frame is already risen, dimmed and cheering, with the card about 2 s after the start. The first celebration frame stalled the UI thread (the first GPU use of the four 1.4x textures and the gradient, stroke and clip pipelines), and the wall-clock `withTiming` ran on through the stall past the rise, which is front-loaded (easeOutBack is 98 % there 160 ms into its 450 ms). Two fixes: the stepped clock (a stall only delays the celebration), and `warmCelebration`, which draws all of it as an invisible speck (each part at 1/255 alpha, the capstone at 2 %) from the moment the style is built (the pre-warm, during play) until the celebration starts, so the GPU work is done before it: 8 tiny draws a frame, only while a tier-10 piece is on the board.
- **Cost per frame** (`render/__tests__/drawCelebration.test.ts`): the board, plus at most 39 draws on Fruits and 117 on Cosmos (1 dim, the lit twinkles, 12 rays or 90 dots, 1 glow, 1 ring, 3-4 bitmaps). Nothing is allocated per frame (the Skia rects for the bitmaps, paths and shaders are made at the win; the paint and the pose are reused); no blur (#3641). No physics runs: the loop has stopped. Not yet measured on a device with the frame readout (#2960).
- **The card.** The shared `GameResultModal` with outcome `win`, as Blackjack's goal-reached result: "You Win!", the score (merge score + time bonus, the server's formula, #3700), New best when it is one, then Merge score, Time bonus, Time (play time, the screen's `PlayClock` at the win) and Best, View leaderboard, Play Again (a fresh game) and Home (`components/cascade/CascadeResultModal.tsx`). The card holds four stats, so the win card leaves out Merges. A loss keeps its card: outcome `ended` ("Game Over"), the score (the merge score), Best, Merges and Time, with no bonus line.
- **Background.** A trip to the background mid-celebration does not restart play (the win tail has ended or resumes the same tail) and the card shows once.
- **Not covered.** `GameResultModal` fades the card in (`animationType="fade"`) whatever Reduce Motion says; that is the shared modal, left as it is here.

- **Activation and cancels.** A native `Pan` activates only after a move (iOS in `touchesMoved`; Android fails an UP while still BEGAN), so a still tap would never drop. The pan uses manual activation and activates itself on the first touch down, on every platform; a second finger fails it (`maxPointers(1)`), so nothing drops at a two-finger centroid. `onEnd`/`onFinalize` report `success: false` when the system takes the gesture over (iOS swipe-back, Android edge-back, a modal, the background, `enabled` flipping): that is a cancel, the piece glides back and nothing drops. The stack's swipe-back is off on the Cascade screen (as on Twenty48 and Mahjong), and the drop path refuses while another screen covers the board. A release the drop path refuses (the cooldown, the lid) glides the piece back like a cancel.
- **Screen readers.** The board keeps its label and gets a hint and an "activate" action (`a11y.boardHint`, `a11y.dropPiece`): a double-tap drops the next piece in the middle, as the old centre tap did.
- **Where it lands.** `aim.ts` `landingY`: the piece falling straight down at the aim x first touches the piece whose contact is highest, `cy − √((r+R)² − dx²)` over every piece under its column (radius × grow-in scale), or the floor. One pass over the packed frame, in the board's Picture worklet, cached on (x, frame, radius) so it runs again only when one of them changes (`render/drawAim.ts`). It uses the drawn radii, so the ghost touches the drawn piece; it shows first contact, not where the piece rolls after, and ignores the engine's 0.4 px drop nudge. The x is clamped as `engine.drop` clamps it (the whole piece between the walls).
- **Thread.** The gesture's callbacks are worklets: they write the aim into the board motion's shared values (`aimState`, `aimX`, `aimAt`, `aimFromX`, `useBoardMotion`), and the motion clock turns a held press into aiming. The JS thread hears only the press (to start the clock), the drag start (the haptic) and the release (the drop). No per-frame JS work while aiming.
- **Keyboard (web, #3934).** The board (`cascade-game-area`) is a tab stop with a visible focus ring (a 3 px border in `colors.accentText`, shown for keyboard focus only, 5.2:1 on the light page and 15:1 on the dark). With it focused: **Left / Right** move the aim one step (`MOTION.aim.keyStep`, 16 world px), **Shift + arrow** a big step (`keyStepBig`, 64), **Space or Enter** drops at the aim and **Escape** cancels it (the piece glides back, nothing drops). The first arrow starts aiming from the spawner's centre and moves one step; the aim stays between the walls (the drop clamps by the piece's radius, as for a drag). `useAimKeyboard` writes the drag's aim shared values, so the guide, the glides and Reduce Motion are the drag's, with no drawing code of its own, and the drop goes through the same `handleTap` (every gate: the cooldown, `canDrop()`, settling, away, game over; a refused aimed drop glides back). Space or Enter with no aim drops in the middle, as the screen reader action does. Arrows still move the aim while a drop is gated (a drag may too); keys do nothing at game over. A pointer's aim is never moved by keys, and losing focus mid-aim cancels it. No haptic. iOS and Android: no listeners and no tab stop. Tests: `useAimKeyboard.test.tsx`, `CascadeAimArea.test.tsx` (focus), `cascade-smoke.spec.ts` (keyboard, with an axe scan).
- **Web.** The gesture reports x on iOS, Android and web (mouse and touch), so the old `offsetX` fallback for web presses went; #3922's rule stays (`boardPxToWorldX`: a non-finite x never drops).
- **Tests.** `aim.test.ts` (tap vs drag, cancel, re-arm, the wall clamp, the cast), `CascadeAimArea.test.tsx` (the gesture through the recorded mock), `drawMotion.test.ts` (the guide, Reduce Motion), `CascadeScreen.motion.test.tsx` (the haptic), `cascade-smoke.spec.ts` (real mouse: a click drops, a drag drops at the moved x, a drag off the board cancels), Maestro `demo-aim.yaml` (a swipe aims and drops; a swipe off the board cancels).

- **Engine.** `land` is the one engine change: a falling piece's first contact records the piece in the contact callback (one push) and `step()` emits `{ type: "land", id, tier, x, y, speed }` after that step, `speed` being its speed before the contact. Nothing else changes: same merges, score and positions, and the sim smoke baseline is unchanged.
- **Thread.** The JS loop pushes the step's moments into a 32-slot ring (`motionLog.ts`) and publishes one copy per frame when something was pushed (`useBoardMotion`). A UI-thread frame callback stamps new slots with its own motion clock (at most 34 ms a frame, so a stall only delays motion), integrates the danger phase, and switches itself off when nothing plays: no frame callback runs on an idle board. The board's Picture closes over these shared values (`worklets.test.ts`) and draws them in `render/drawMotion.ts` (worklets, no blur or gradient, no path built per frame, one reused paint). Peak cost is a 4-chain: about 56 extra draws for the pops plus the counter.
- **Chain** means the merges since the last drop (the screen counts them, `chainLinkRef`): the first is a plain merge, the second shows ×2, and so on. A merge while a restored game settles or in a test fast-forward is not counted and ends the chain.
- **Danger blink phase.** The phase never resets: when the count stops during an on-beat the beat finishes (at the fastest rate), then the phase waits at the start of an off-beat. An on-beat only starts when the phase wraps, so onsets are at least 340 ms apart however often a merge pauses and restarts the count.
- **Haptics** (`haptics.ts`, expo-haptics, best effort as `GameResultModal`'s, none on web, no setting): land Light (Medium from tier 7); merge Medium (tiers 0-4), Heavy (5-7), Heavy twice 50 ms apart (8-10; shifted by one tier with the 12-tier ladder so every existing piece keeps its feel, #3962); chain links Light, Medium, then Heavy on the counter's paced steps (in place of a merge pulse); one Warning when the lid count passes 0.5 (again only after it cleared); the win's Success is the card's (as in Sort, so it fires once). Paced: never two within 40 ms, at most 6 waiting, landings within 60 ms dropped, every pulse fired from a timer, none while a restored game settles or for a test fast-forward.
- **Reduce Motion** is read once by the screen through the shared `useReduceMotion` (#2984) and passed to the board and the preview. On the Android Maestro emulator animations are off, so the demo flows take this path.
- **Proposals.** The three clips and their tables are on #3701.

## Game over: the lid

The lid is the line at `OVERFLOW_LINE_Y` (y = 80 of the 600-unit world). The rule is in `engine.ts` (#3694) and is built so that it always fires, however fast the player taps. Every count is in fixed physics steps (`FIXED_STEP_MS`, 60 per second), never frames or wall-clock time, so it runs equivalently at 60 Hz and 120 Hz (same simulated time to within one frame; the per-step restructure keeps 60 Hz behaviour equivalent to before, not bit-identical). Frames slower than 20 fps drop their backlog (`step` caps the accumulator at `MAX_SUBSTEPS` steps), so game time then runs slower than the wall clock instead of building a queue.

- **Overflow.** A piece counts when it is _settled_ (its speed at most `OVERFLOW_SETTLED_SPEED` = 8 px per step) and its **top edge** (y − radius) is above the lid. A dropped piece still falling from the spawner never counts, until it first touches another piece or the floor. While at least one piece counts, a counter rises by one each step; a step with none resets it to 0. At `OVERFLOW_TICKS_THRESHOLD` (180 steps, 3 s) the game ends (`gameOver`, reason `overflow`).
- **Merge grace.** A merge involving a piece whose top edge is in the danger band (at or above `OVERFLOW_LINE_Y + DANGER_STACK_MARGIN`, y = 160) resets the counter and pauses it for `OVERFLOW_IGNORE_MERGE_TICKS` (60 steps, 1 s). A merge lower in the bin does nothing. There is no other grace or cooldown. (Before #3694 any merge anywhere reset the counter, so fast play never lost.)
- **Hard stop.** A settled piece whose top edge reaches the top of the board (y ≤ `HARD_STOP_Y` = 0) ends the game at once (reason `hardStop`), so the pile is never drawn off-screen. The last dropped piece gets a short grace: a large piece spawns with its top edge above the canvas (since #3697 droppable pieces spawn on it: on the #3920 ladder the Apple's top edge is at y = 8; a dev-panel piece of tier 6 and up spawns above it) and can touch a pile that reaches just under the lid while still near spawn height, so it must stay settled with its top edge at or above y = 0 for `HARD_STOP_SETTLE_TICKS` (30 steps, 0.5 s) before the hard stop fires. If it really rests there, the game still ends (and the overflow count runs meanwhile).
- **Drop gating.** `canDrop()` is false from a drop until that piece is below the lid (its top edge at or under the line), or merges into a piece that is. The screen ignores taps until then (on top of its 200 ms tap debounce). It cannot deadlock: once the piece lands it counts like any other, so a piece that comes to rest over the lid ends the game through the overflow rule. `drop()` itself is not gated, so the dev panel and the `__cascade_*` test seams can still place pieces.
- **Restore.** A restored game gets the merge grace once (1 s while the pieces settle), then the counter runs as normal. It is one grace per restore: a later restore starts a fresh grace, but does not extend a count already running. The hard stop applies at once to restored pieces that are part of the pile. A saved piece whose top edge is over the lid and that touches no other piece was most likely mid-air (velocities are not saved), so it is treated as falling again (no hard stop or overflow count until its first contact); if exactly one such piece exists it is the last drop for `canDrop()` and the hard-stop settle run.
- **Danger state.** `getState().dangerRatio` is the counter over the threshold (0..1) and `getState().canDrop` the gate. The board draws them (`render/drawFrame.ts`, mappings in `lidVisuals.ts`, colours `CASCADE_LID_PALETTE` in `theme/theme.cascade.ts`): a solid lid line that reddens as the counter runs; a clock ring around the spawner piece (above the lid) that drains clockwise from cyan to red, with a glow from 0.7; a red wash and outline on pieces over the lid while the counter runs; and the spawner piece as a dashed, faded ghost while a drop is not allowed.
- **Regression.** `__tests__/engine.sim.test.ts` runs a bot tapping every 200–250 ms at random x on fixed seeds; it must lose within 120 simulated seconds at 60 and 120 Hz. On #3920's ladder, physics and danger rules it loses in 48–72 s (seeds 1–5: 65.4, 55.3, 60.6, 68.5, 72.0 s at 60 Hz; 57.3, 48.2, 71.8, 51.8, 54.6 s at 120 Hz) and never wins. The longest of the 100-seed suite is 84.9 s at 60 Hz and 84.4 s at 120 Hz, so the bound has about 40 % headroom (#3904: 20 %, longest 99.3 / 100.0 s; #3697: 14 %; Matter.js: 1.6x).
- **Danger rules and mashing (#3904).** The lid's countdown (3 s) and merge grace (1 s) are unchanged; the spawner's danger mode changed twice. Shortening the countdown or the grace could not end a mashing game sooner: in the one spam game that outlived the bound on the first #3904 tuning (seed 72 at 60 Hz) the pile first reaches the lid at about 113 s and keeps dipping back under it, so even a 1.5 s countdown with no merge grace left it alive at 120 s. What kept it alive was danger mode's boost of the small tiers (x1.5 for tiers 0-2), which fed a near-lid pile a stream of easy merges; #3904 removed it. Review then found that the penalty on the big tiers rounded and floored at weight 1, which left tier 7 (weight 1) unpenalised; a plain x0.2 on tiers 3-7 fixed that but left the spam bot's longest game at 116.4 s. So tiers 3-6 are scaled by `DANGER_PENALTY` (0.2) and the top tier, the Coconut / Uranus, by its own milder `DANGER_PENALTY_TOP` (0.35, owner decision on PR #3919). Sweep on the 100-seed spam suite, longest game at 60 / 120 Hz (no spam game won in any): x0.2 110.0 / 116.4 s; **x0.35 99.3 / 100.0 s**; x0.5 100.7 / 96.8 s; x0.7 90.5 / 85.0 s. The skilled bot won 50 % at both 0.35 and 0.5 (heuristic 30 % / 29 %). 0.35 keeps the top tier clearly rarer in danger than calm (6.4 % of draws against 8.0 %; at 0.5 it is 7.8 %, a hair under, and at 0.7 it would come more often in danger); `sim.test.ts` asserts it is under 90 % of its calm share. A careful player barely sees danger: the skilled bot drops in danger mode for 9.6 % of its drops, and the lid's countdown runs in 50 of 100 games, all of them lost, 1.3 times a game, 0.7 % of the time; the heuristic bot never reached the danger band in 100 games of 180 s. The win rates moved within sampling error (skilled 52 % before the danger changes, 50 % after; heuristic 26 % to 30 %). The danger ring is unchanged (it shows the same 3 s count). **#3920** kept the same intent on its 0-5 drops: tiers 2-4 x0.2 and the top tier (5, Apple / Neptune) x0.35, unrounded. With the single-capstone win and drops 0-5 the spam bot loses by 84.9 / 84.4 s and never wins (drops 0-6 let it win 3 games of 100, one reason the owner chose 0-5); the top tier comes 8.4 % of draws in danger against 10.2 % calm.

## Physics

The owner chose **"3 lively"** of the three planck.js proposals (#3697; the spike #3696 and the proposals: [`CASCADE_PHYSICS_SPIKE.md`](../research/CASCADE_PHYSICS_SPIKE.md), [`CASCADE_FEEL_PROPOSALS.md`](../research/CASCADE_FEEL_PROPOSALS.md)). It replaced Matter.js, which teleported pieces back out of the walls about 1,900 times a minute and sank resting pieces 2-3 px into each other.

- **Engine:** planck.js (Box2D in JavaScript), pinned at exactly `1.4.2` in `frontend/package.json`; rendered via `@shopify/react-native-skia`. `physicsWorld.ts` owns the planck `World` and converts at its boundary: 50 px per metre (radii 0.23-1.96 m on the #3920 ladder, inside Box2D's 0.1-10 m), velocities in px per fixed step, angles in radians. Pieces are true circles. planck 1.4.2's polygon mass bug (a dynamic polygon's centre of mass; fixed in 1.5) does not apply: Cascade has only dynamic circles and static boxes. 1.4.2 and 1.5.1 played the spike's 600 games bit for bit the same.
- **Tuning (lively, retuned by #3904):** friction 0.12 (pieces, walls and floor; was 0.2), restitution 0.2, today's gravity (2500 px/s²), air friction 0.01 per step as Box2D linear damping, no rolling damping (`PIECE_ANGULAR_DAMPING` = 0) with spin clamped at `MAX_ANGULAR_VELOCITY`, density `(r0 / r)^0.5` (`tiers.ts`). The solver: each fixed step is `PLANCK_STEPS_PER_FIXED_STEP` (2) planck steps of 8.33 ms, each with 10 velocity / 20 position iterations (#3697: one step, 10 / 8), and planck's Baumgarte factor 0.35 (`POSITION_BAUMGARTE`; planck's default 0.2). The grow-in is 16 steps (was 8). All in `constants.ts`; [Overlap](#overlap-3904) has why.
- **Timestep:** fixed 16.67 ms (60 Hz) with up to 3 fixed steps per frame, each two planck steps; every rule runs once per fixed step, so 60 and 120 Hz play the same rules over the same simulated time.
- **Walls:** 200 px thick, grown outward from the bin's inner faces, and 600 px above the canvas, so nothing tunnels out. **No guard rail in normal play**: nothing is teleported. The only safety left catches a piece that escaped the bin outright (its centre a radius past a wall's or the floor's inner face, or a non-finite position): it is put back and stopped, `guardRailFired` (`escaped`) is emitted, and the screen logs it in dev builds. The sim gate requires 0. Bodies are only removed by a merge.
- **Merges:** two same-tier pieces merge when a planck contact begins between them, or when their circles are within `MERGE_GAP_PX` (1.5 px) at the end of a step (Box2D rests pieces a fraction of a pixel apart, which looks like touching). The scan runs per tier over the same-tier pieces only and does not allocate. The merged piece is made at the parents' midpoint, **clamped so its full-size circle is inside the walls and above the floor**, with the parents' mean velocity (capped at `MAX_SPAWN_VELOCITY`) and a `MERGE_POP_IMPULSE` (0.4 px per step) kick upward.
- **Grow-in:** the merged piece starts at its parents' radius and grows to its own over `GROW_IN_STEPS` (16 steps, 267 ms; 8 before #3904, which on the larger top tiers grew faster than the solver could move the neighbours aside); each step keeps the grown circle inside the bin (a shift of at most one grow step, never a velocity change). Box2D resolves the overlap with a neighbour by position, so a neighbour is moved aside, not shot out (`engine.physics.test.ts` checks no neighbour passes 1.5 px per step). The snapshot carries the piece's `scale` (current / full radius), and the renderer draws it at that size. The lid, the merge grace and the drop gate always use the full radius.
- **Chain reach:** a piece still growing in also merges with a same-tier piece its full radius would touch (`CHAIN_REACH_PX` = 2), without waiting to grow into it, so a chain runs one step per link. Since #3920 it is checked where the product is made, in the step it is made (the merge runs the next step), as well as each step it grows: a product made at its parents' size on top of a third piece can be shoved several px by the solver in its first step (up to 12 px seen in the sim), out of reach of the same-tier piece it was made touching, and the chain was lost (about 2-2.5 % of watched chains on #3904's ladder, 1-1.5 % on this one before the fix; see [Sim harness](#sim-harness-sim-2893)).
- **The win:** making the capstone (#3920), see [The win](#the-win-3920).
- **Sleep:** planck's own (a body at rest for 0.5 s sleeps). The post-physics pass (reading positions, the spin clamp, the safety) skips a body already checked while asleep (#2960): planck neither moves nor spins it.
- **Drop nudge.** `drop(tier, x)` moves the piece by a seeded amount in +-0.4 px (`DROP_NUDGE_PX`) before the wall clamp, so a piece dropped at the same x as the one below it does not land dead centre and stack into a column that never topples. The engine owns the RNG (`EngineConfig.seed` / `seed(n)`; the screen seeds it from the clock, `__cascade_setSeed` reseeds it), one draw per drop. `bench.noDropNudge` switches it off for the lid tests, which land a drop on a restored column.
- **Determinism:** no unseeded randomness anywhere in the engine or planck's solver (the drop nudge is a seeded draw), and piece ids count from 1 per engine, so the same frame deltas replay the same board, bit for bit, at 60 and at 120 Hz (each rate against itself; replays, #3858). 60 and 120 Hz are not bit-identical to each other: they play the same fixed steps and match within a frame (the lid tests). Bit-exact replay holds on one JS engine only: planck calls `Math.sin`, `Math.cos` and `Math.atan2`, which can differ in the last bit between Hermes, JavaScriptCore and V8, so a replay on another device, or on a server, of a chaotic pile can diverge. Do not build anything that needs the same board across engines on it.
- **Allocation:** the engine's own step path (the merge scan, grow-in, chain reach, the post-physics pass and `getState(into)`) allocates nothing per piece. planck's internals do allocate: about 0.4 KB per frame with the pile settled and about 2 KB per frame with 90 awake pieces (measured in review of #3863). The device bench (`Step bench` in the dev panel) is the check, not this note.
- **Events:** `merge` (now with the new piece's `id` and its `parentIds`), `score`, `cascadeCombo`, `gameOver`, `win`, and the dev-only `guardRailFired`.

| the same, with #3697's danger boost of small tiers (round 1)                                                                                                                                                                                                        | 52 %        | 2.5 / 5.2 (1.7 / 4.9 drawn)                  | loses by 120.0 (seed 72 alive at the cap) / 107.7 s |
| **shipped by #3904**: two planck steps per fixed step, 10 / 20 iterations, Baumgarte 0.35, friction 0.12, grow-in 16, density 0.5; danger mode x0.2 on tiers 3-6 and x0.35 on tier 7 (superseded by #3920: x0.2 on tiers 2-4, x0.35 on tier 5), no small-tier boost | **50 %**    | 2.4 / 5.2 (**1.6 / 4.9** drawn 1.5 % inside) | loses by 99.3 / 100.0 s; never wins                 |

What the numbers say: the overlap was free room. Every lever that made pieces sit apart also made the pile taller, and the skilled bot's losses are all the lid (under #3904's two-capstone win it made its first capstone in nearly every game, then needed about 100 s for the second). So the density exponent, the owner's first lever, cost the most win rate for the least overlap (heavy pieces sinking through light ones is also what packs the pile), and stays at 0.5. Sub-stepping is what removes the overlap of small pieces squeezed under a deep pile (the tail of the distribution); lower friction gives some of the packing back, but below about 0.12 the spam bot starts to outlive the lid or win. The draw inset (1.5 %) is small: 0.35 px between two cherries on #3920's ladder (0.15 px on #3904's), 2.9 px between two capstones, a mean visible gap of +0.6 to +0.8 px.

The rest of a game's deepest overlap is transients: a merged piece appearing at its parents' size on a piece in the valley between them (one or two frames, pushed out by the solver) and a fast drop landing.

**The spam bot and the lid.** Every setting above sat on a knife edge between three of the owner's limits: the skilled win rate (at least 45 %), the overlap gates, and the spam bot (loses within 120 s, never wins). Friction could not settle it (0.14 ended the spam games and cost the win rate and the overlap). The fix was a danger rule instead: see [Danger rules and mashing](#game-over-the-lid). The win rates move by about 5 points from one 100-seed set to the next (sampling error), so 50 % here is "about half".

## Rendering (#2960)

Nothing per frame goes through React state. The screen's RAF loop steps the engine and reads it with `engine.getState(into)`, which writes into one reused state object (the read allocates nothing per frame; planck's step does allocate a little, see Allocation under Physics). `render/frame.ts` packs what the board draws into a `Float64Array`: a header (piece count, `dangerRatio`, `canDrop`, the spawner's tier) and seven numbers per piece (id, tier, x, y, angle, sleeping, and since #3697 scale: a merged piece's size while it grows in). The loop publishes a copy to a Reanimated shared value only when it differs from the last one published. `components/cascade/CascadeBoard.tsx` records a Skia `Picture` from that shared value in `useDerivedValue` (`createPicture`), on the UI thread, with `render/drawFrame.ts`; the Picture is drawn inside a `Group` scaled from world units to the board.

- **What is drawn.** The same primitives, in the same order and with the same paints, as the declarative `PieceRenderer` and the #3694 lid components it replaced: each piece (its three cached art bitmaps at the ladder radius, see [Art (#3698)](#art-3698), or a placeholder circle) with its over-lid wash, then the lid line, then the spawner (piece, waiting ghost, clock ring). Every lid decision comes from `lidVisuals.ts`, whose mappings are worklets. A headless CanvasKit comparison of the old and new renderers on real engine states (light and dark, sprites and placeholders, calm, waiting, danger 0.5 and 0.9) was pixel-identical. That comparison ran headless, on one thread (CanvasKit, with the Picture recorded and drawn on the JS thread); it has not been verified on a device, where the Picture is recorded on the UI thread. Checking the board on iOS and Android is an owner follow-up (the Maestro screenshots of #3705, or a device session). `render/__tests__/worklets.test.ts` runs the real worklets Babel plugin to check that every function the UI-thread recording reaches is a worklet, which the Jest Reanimated stub cannot.
- **Sleeping pieces** are drawn where they fell asleep (the #1746 freeze, kept in the packer). A settled board therefore packs the same frame every time and publishes nothing.
- **Score.** `scoreStore.ts` holds the live score; `ScoreDisplay` subscribes with `useSyncExternalStore` and re-renders only when the integer changes. `ScoreDisplay`, `ThemeSelector`, `NextFruitPreview` and `CascadeBoard` are `React.memo`; the screen's handlers and styles are hoisted.
- **Events.** Merges and game over still reach the screen through `onMergeRef` / `onGameOverRef`; a merge burst (`components/cascade/MergeBurst.tsx`) is the one React update a merge makes, besides the score (none for the winning merge, #3699).
- **The win celebration (#3699)** is drawn into the same Picture from a second shared value, its clock: see [The celebration and the win card](#the-celebration-and-the-win-card-3699).
- **Measured.** `CascadeScreen.frames.test.tsx` counts React commits with a `Profiler`: 60 frames commit 60 times before #2960 (settled or moving) and 0 times after; a score change commits once. The dev panel's _Frame_ line (Star Swarm's `render/frameStats.ts` ring buffer, dev builds only) shows frame time and commits per second on a device; commits are counted by a `Profiler` around the screen, so a commit of `ScoreDisplay` alone counts. Numbers are in [PERFORMANCE.md](../PERFORMANCE.md#cascade-renderer-2960).
- **Web (CanvasKit, #2960 part b).** On web the same Skia code draws the board, through Skia's web runtime: CanvasKit, Skia compiled to WebAssembly. Owner decision 2026-11-11 on #2960: one drawing path on every platform, so web demos (the only demos a cloud session can record itself) show what the phones show; a separate Canvas 2D board like Mahjong's would draw everything twice and drift. How it loads:
  - `lazyScreens.ts` routes Cascade to `screens/CascadeRoute`. On iOS and Android `CascadeRoute.tsx` is the screen itself. On web Metro picks `CascadeRoute.web.tsx`, which shows a loading state, calls `loadCascadeScreen.web.ts` (CanvasKit first, then `import("./CascadeScreen")` in its own chunk) and renders the screen. The order matters: `react-native-skia`'s web module builds its `Skia` API from `global.CanvasKit` when it is first evaluated, so nothing that imports Skia may be evaluated earlier. This is the pattern of Skia's `WithSkiaWeb`, written out so a failure can be retried.
  - `game/cascade/render/loadCanvasKit.ts` mirrors Skia's `LoadSkiaWeb`, with two changes: the `.wasm` comes from Metro's asset pipeline (`require` of `canvaskit-wasm/bin/full/canvaskit.wasm`; `.wasm` is in `metro.config.js` `assetExts`), so the web export writes it to `dist/assets/node_modules/canvaskit-wasm/bin/full/canvaskit.<hash>.wasm` and it is served from our own origin, never a CDN; and a failed load is forgotten, so Retry tries again (`LoadSkiaWeb` keeps a rejected promise). `canvaskit-wasm` is a direct dependency pinned to the version `@shopify/react-native-skia` depends on (0.41.0 for Skia 2.14.0); `render/__tests__/canvasKitWeb.test.ts` fails if they drift, or if a non-web file imports the loader.
  - A load that fails or takes longer than 60 s (`CANVASKIT_LOAD_TIMEOUT_MS`) shows a readable error with Try again inside the game shell (`cascade-web-error`), never an endless spinner (#2539). Only the first failure in a page session reports a Sentry event (`subsystem: cascade.canvaskit`); later ones leave a breadcrumb. Retry after a timeout starts a fresh download. A browser that blocks WebAssembly under its Content-Security-Policy gets the same error: the deployed dev site's CSP allows `'wasm-unsafe-eval'` for this (`render.yaml`; prod gets it in the change that shows Cascade in the store web build, see [RENDER.md](../RENDER.md)).
  - Nothing else loads CanvasKit: the lobby and the other web games never request the `.wasm`, and Mahjong and Star Swarm keep their Canvas 2D `GameCanvas.web.tsx` (the Playwright spec checks both). The iOS and Android bundles hold neither the loader nor the `.wasm` (checked on an Android export, #2960 part b). Download cost: [PERFORMANCE.md](../PERFORMANCE.md#cascade-on-web-canvaskit-2960).
  - Board accessibility: the board's role and label sit on a `View` around the Skia `Canvas`, because Skia's web `Canvas` drops accessibility props.
  - Skia features the renderer uses, checked on web (Chromium, WebGL): `createPicture` / `Picture` inside a scaled `Group`, `drawCircle`, `drawLine`, `drawImageRectOptions` with the cached piece bitmaps (`pieceSprites`), `Path.MakeFromSVGString` + `drawPath`, `PathEffect.MakeDash` (the waiting ghost) and `MaskFilter.MakeBlur` (the clock ring's glow). All draw on web; the Picture is recorded on the JS thread there (Reanimated runs worklets on the JS thread on web). The art (#3698) uses gradients, but only when it rasterises a bitmap, on the JS thread (CanvasKit's CPU surface on web); the per-frame Picture draws bitmaps only. Motion (#3701) adds `drawRect`, stroked `drawPath` of cached paths, `PathEffect.MakeDash` (cached per offset) and transforms only; checked on web (Playwright recording on the PR), a phone check is part of its device demo. Not yet compared side by side with a phone screenshot (the Maestro screenshots of #3705).
  - E2E: `e2e/tests/cascade-smoke.spec.ts` (Playwright project `cascade`).

## Art (#3698)

All 24 pieces are drawn in code (and the retired ones are kept as recipes, see [Retired pieces](#retired-pieces-3920)); Cascade has no image files. The owner chose the style on 2026-11-14 from three proposals (issue #3698): **kawaii glossy sticker**. The art is original to the project (see [ASSETS.md](../ASSETS.md)).

- **Style.** A glossy gradient body, a darker rim just inside the edge, outlined features, two specular dots and a face. Fruits have open eyes and a smile; cosmos pieces have closed eyes and blush, so pieces that share a hue (Pluto, Mercury; Neptune, Uranus) also differ by marks. #3920 made the Apple a bright glossy green (`CASCADE_ART.fruits.apple`), so it no longer reads as the Cherry; it stays apart from the Lemon (yellow) and the Watermelon (dark, striped). Only paths, linear and radial gradients, circles and round-capped strokes are used: no `BlurMask`, no blur, no bitmaps, no shader per frame. Colours are tokens in `CASCADE_ART` (`src/theme/theme.cascade.ts`), so the colour gate sees no literals in the art code.
- **Modules** (`frontend/src/game/cascade/art/`).
  - `pen.ts`: how a piece is painted (the gradients, outline, specular dots, face) and the three layers. A piece says what is on it, in unit space: centre (0, 0), collider radius 1, y down.
  - `fruits.ts`: the twelve Fruits, the Lychee, Lime, Plum and Pomegranate (#3964) and the Dragonfruit (tier 8 since #3960, take B) included, and the eight retired recipes (Peach, red Apple, Blueberry, Grape, Coconut, Fig B, Fig C, Mangosteen B).
  - `cosmos.ts`: the twelve Cosmos pieces (the Moon is back at tier 1, #3960); nothing is retired there now.
  - `sprites.ts`: the caches, and `piecePicture` for icons.
  - `bin.ts`: the bin (see Bin below).
  - Adding or changing a piece edits `fruits.ts` or `cosmos.ts` only; run `UPDATE_RASTER_REFS=1 npx jest art.test` to rewrite the reference sheet and review the image diff.
- **Drawn radius.** The body is clipped to the unit circle and drawn at `tierDrawnRadius(tier)` from `tiers.ts`: the ladder radius less the 1.5 % draw inset (#3904). There is no padded bitmap and no other scale factor between the art and the physics circle. `art.test.ts` checks that the body fills the drawn circle to its edge and that nothing outside it is drawn except the declared overhangs. On the #3967 ladder (R0 = 11) the Cherry is drawn 21.7 px across in world units (about 21 pt on a 390 pt phone, 63 bitmap pixels at 3x; 22.7 px on #3920's, 9.6 px on #3904's). The bitmap cache copes with the 11-98 range as it is: one bitmap pixel per screen pixel at each tier's drawn radius, `pxPerUnit` unchanged.
- **Caching: three layers, light upright, faces roll.** Each tier is three CPU bitmaps (`pieceSprites(setId, pxPerUnit)`): `body` (back overhangs and the clipped body; rotates with the piece), `light` (the rim shade, gloss and specular dots; upright, so the highlight stays top-left however the piece rolls) and `top` (the face and the front overhangs; rotates). The face rolls with the body, as a sticker on it would (owner, 2026-11-14); an upright face over a rolling body slides across features such as the coconut's eyes. The board draws each piece as three `drawImageRect`s. The bitmap scale is `pxPerUnit` = board scale x device pixel ratio, so a piece is one bitmap pixel per screen pixel at its full radius and is never scaled up (only down, for the grow-in and the spawner's copy). Cache keys are the set and `pxPerUnit` rounded up to 0.05 (so a pixel-or-two resize reuses the set); the last 3 are kept and an evicted key's bitmaps are disposed (`MAX_SPRITE_SETS`). Icons (next-piece preview, theme selector, dev panel, home tile) use `piecePicture(set, tier, size)`: one upright vector `SkPicture` per set, tier and size, sharp at any pixel ratio. A tier that cannot be rasterised stays `null` and the board draws the placeholder circle.
- **Sizes.** Art code: 17.4 KB minified (7.2 KB gzipped) for both sets before #3964, under the 20 KB budget. #3964 (the four new Fruits and six more retired recipes) adds about 6.4 KB minified, 2.2 KB gzipped, so about 23.8 KB, over the 20 KB design target (no test enforces it; the owner decides whether to keep it, PR #3984). Measured wider (the art, its palette `theme.cascade.ts` and the sprite caches as bundled by esbuild) the same change is 20.9 to 27.3 KB, 8.6 to 10.8 KB gzipped: the same +6.4 KB on a larger base, so that scope was already above 20 KB before this change. ## Piece Queue & Spawn Selection (`pieceQueue.ts`, `spawnSelector.ts`)

- Queue holds `{ current, next }` tiers; `advanceQueue()` shifts forward and generates a new `next`
- Tier selection uses a weighted random algorithm:
  1. Base weights over tiers 0-5 (`1 1 2 3 4 4`, #3967, for the 12-tier ladder): a drop carries about 15.1 Cherries' worth of material, so the win (one capstone, 2048 Cherries) is about 135 perfect drops away, as on the 11-tier ladder (#3920's 11 tiers: `2 3 3 3 2 1`, 7.7 Cherries a drop, 133 drops; #3904's V1: tiers 0-7 weighted `2 3 3 3 3 3 2 1`, 22.2 Cherries a drop, two capstones to win; #3697: 0-5 weighted `2 3 3 3 3 2`)
  2. Drought boost: tiers absent from the queue's last 10 picks (DROP and NEXT included) gain +3 weight
  3. Danger mode: when the stack is near the overflow line, tiers 2-4 are scaled to 20% of their weight and the top tier (5) to 25% (#3967; 35% on #3920's weights, where tier 5 had weight 1: at weight 4, x0.35 left it as common in danger as calm, 24.3% against 24.1%, so x0.25 makes it 19.4%; #3920; on #3904's ladder tiers 3-6 and 7, the same intent; fractional; #3697 rounded and kept at least weight 1, which left the weight-1 tier 7 unpenalised; the milder top-tier factor is the owner's PR #3919 decision, see [Danger rules and mashing](#game-over-the-lid)). (#3697 also multiplied tiers 0-2 by 1.5; #3904 removed that, see [Danger rules and mashing](#game-over-the-lid).)
  4. Streak hard-ban: if the last 4 picks are identical, that tier is banned for one turn
- RNG is injectable for deterministic testing and seeded replay. Since #3858 live play is seeded too: each game draws a queue seed and an engine seed (`useCascadeQueue`, `newSeeds`), the queue draws from the queue seed (`pieceQueue.createSeededRng`, the generator the `__cascade_setSeed` test seam uses) and the engine's drop nudge from the engine seed; both are recorded in `game_started`. The draws are uniform like `Math.random`'s, so play is unchanged

### Balance pass for the 12-tier ladder (#3967)

Target (owner, #3961): skilled bot about 50-55 %, heuristic about 30-35 %, median skilled win about 4-5 min. Fixed: R0 = 11.5 and capstone 98 (K derived), tiers 0-5 droppable, score `2^n`, no density flattening (#3904). Levers in order: (a) spawn and danger weights, (b) lid timing if a tuning constant, (c) the capstone radius and / or R0. The lid's 3 s count, its 1 s merge grace and the y = 160 danger band are rules in the specification ([cascade.md section 6](cascade.md#6-danger-and-the-lid)), so (b) was not used.

## Combo Detection (`engine.ts`)

A `cascadeCombo` event is emitted when ≥3 merges occur within `COMBO_WINDOW_TICKS` (120 ticks, ~2 s). The combo counter resets on each drop. The window is counted in fixed physics steps, so it is 2 s of simulated time at any frame rate (it used to be counted per frame: 2 s at 60 Hz, 1 s at 120 Hz).

The screen does not consume `cascadeCombo`: nothing in `CascadeScreen` or the components listens for it. What the player sees as a combo is the chain counter (merges since the last drop, [Motion and haptics](#motion-and-haptics-3701)), which the screen counts itself. The event is engine API and test surface only.

## Sim harness (`sim/`, #2893)

A seeded, headless sim in `frontend/src/game/cascade/sim/` plays the real `CascadeEngine`, spawn selector and queue (`createPieceQueue` / `advanceQueue`, fed by the shared seeded RNG) the way `CascadeScreen` does: taps are gated by `canDrop()`, `isInDanger` comes from the pile height. It is the measuring tool for the physics spike (#3696), the physics story (#3697), the lid (#3694) and the tier ladder (#3695). Same seed, bot, frame rate and engine give the same metrics (step cost aside).

- **Bots** (`bots.ts`): `heuristic` (waits for a calm board, drops where it lands on a same-tier piece, else lowest), `random` (600-1200 ms, random x), `spam` (200-250 ms, random x; the lid test's bot), `skilled` (#3697: the heuristic's timing, but it keeps the pile graded, big pieces left and small right, sets up merges next to a piece one tier away and avoids burying small pieces; the capstone runs' bot). The sim test compares the heuristic and random bots by merges per drop, not by score: on the #3904 ladder the random bot survives the 180 s cap in almost every game and drops about a quarter more pieces (it does not wait for a calm board), so its total score is higher although it merges less per drop (0.58 against 0.74).
- **Frame rates:** every run is done at 60 and 120 Hz frame deltas.
- **Metrics** (`runGame.ts`, `suite.ts`): piece frequency per tier; drought (longest run of drops without a tier); game length (simulated s), score, score by tier, top-tier reached rate; win rate and time to win (the engine's `win` event, #3697; making the capstone since #3920), the time to the win (p25 / median / p75 of the games that won) and to the tier below the capstone (`preCapstone.timeS.median`, tier 10 since #3960; tier 9 on the 11-tier ladder, `tier10` before); every run ends at the win, since play ends there (#3920; only the capstone runs did before); physics feel: packing density, visible gap between touching neighbours (drawn radius), settle time after a drop and after a 3+ merge chain, jitter of settled pieces (px/s), guard-rail events per game (`guardRailFired`, counted, never acted on: #1716); wall penetration (deepest, and pieces more than 1 px in per minute, read every frame at each piece's current size); chain merge latency (a merge product touching a same-tier piece at full size when made, timed until it merges again, from the merge events' `id` / `parentIds`; one not merged within 1 s counts as a miss at 1000 ms; a watch still open at the win is dropped, since nothing merges after it); step cost (wall clock, printed but not in the baseline). Hard gates (not bands): the spam bot always reaches game over inside 120 simulated seconds and never wins (#3904), no guard rail fires, chain-merge latency p90 is under 150 ms, each run's deepest overlap is at most 3 px median over its games (and at most 5 px p90 in runs of 100 games) with a mean visible gap of at least -0.5 px (#3904, see [Overlap](#overlap-3904)), and the mean step with 20+ pieces is under 16 ms.
- **1 px-contact spawn helper** (`contactSpawn.ts`): `contactPair(tier, x)` places two same-tier pieces overlapped by 1 px, for merge tests (planck's circles are exact; on Matter it added a polygon-facet allowance). Deep overlaps can separate before `collisionStart` fires and the merge silently fails (#1296, #1302, #1418).
- **Comparing a candidate** (#3696): `runGame` / `runSuite` take a `SimConfig`: `createEngine` (a factory for any engine with the `SimEngine` surface) and `constants` (overrides for what the sim and its metrics read: world size, lid y, physics and drawn radius per tier, score per tier, droppable tiers). The real engine reads its tuning from `constants.ts` at import time, so a candidate engine brings its own numbers and the same numbers go in `constants`.
- **Run it:** `cd frontend && npm run sim:cascade` (smoke, 3 seeds, about 30 s) or `npm run sim:cascade -- --full` (100 seeds x 3 bots x 60/120 Hz plus the capstone runs, 100 skilled games at 60 Hz of up to 10 simulated minutes each that stop at the win; about 30 min since #3904 (12 before; about 18 min in this container on #3920's ladder); `--no-capstone` skips them). `--gate` checks `sim/baseline.json` and exits 1 on a violation; `--update-baseline` rewrites the baseline for that suite (then `npx prettier --write` the file); `--md PATH` / `--json PATH` write the table / raw result.
- **Baseline and bands:** `sim/baseline.json` holds today's deterministic metrics with a band per metric (relative plus an absolute floor per unit; rates never tighter than one game of n). Bands are drift guards around today's physics, not targets; #3695 / #3697 re-baselined. The smoke suite is a coarse guard: it bands only game length, score and guard-rail rate with wide tolerances (checked with the seed set shifted), and the lid and step budget are hard gates; per-tier rates, shares, droughts and settle are banded in the full suite only, which assumes 100 seeds (a run with another seed count prints the table and skips the bands).
- **Settle time is survivor-biased.** A drop that arrives before the board calms cuts that episode off, and it never reaches the mean. The table prints the cut-off fraction next to it (`settle episodes cut off by the next drop`): about 0.5 for the heuristic and random bots, 0.99 for spam. So the **spam bot's settle numbers are not comparable** (they come from a few unusual episodes) and are never banded; the heuristic and random settle means are banded only when 20 or more episodes finished. Compare settle between engines for the same bot and read it with its cut-off fraction. Speed thresholds (calm 18 px/s, settled 30 px/s) are per second, so 60 and 120 Hz compare.
- **CI:** the smoke runs in every PR's Jest run (`sim/__tests__/sim.test.ts`, about 8 s); the long run is `.github/workflows/cascade-sim-gate.yml` (nightly, on demand, and on PRs that touch the engine, its constants, the queue or the sim). PR runs play 50 capstone seeds (about 7 min before #3904, about 2.4x since) instead of 100, with the capstone bands widened by sqrt(100 / 50) (`--capstone-seeds`, `baselineForCapstoneSeeds`); nightly and manual runs play 100.

## Persistence (`storage.ts`)

- Saved state is versioned (`SavedState` v8, key `cascade_game_v8`) — includes pieces, score, and queue state
- A v7 save (#3920's 11-tier ladder, key `cascade_game_v7`) is **discarded**, not migrated (#3960): every tier above 0 is now another piece or another size (the Moon is back at tier 1 and a Lime / Mercury sits at tier 2, so a saved tier 5 would come back as a different piece at a different radius, and a saved tier 10 would be one merge short of the capstone), so its board would be other pieces, overlapping. Its key is removed on load and on clear; a relaunch with one starts a fresh game
- A v6 save (#3904's 12-tier ladder, key `cascade_game_v6`) is **discarded**, not migrated (#3920): every tier above 0 is now another piece (the Peach and the Moon are gone, so a saved tier 6 would come back as a Coconut, and tier 11 no longer exists) and every radius changed (Cherry 5 to 11.5). Its key is removed on load and on clear
- A v5 save (#3697's lively ladder, key `cascade_game_v5`) is **discarded**, not migrated (#3904): every radius changed (Cherry 18 to 5, Watermelon 56 to 74.8, the capstone 63 to 98), so its positions would overlap or leave gaps, and a single capstone in it is now half a win. Its key is removed on load and on clear
- A v4 save (#3695's ladder on Matter.js, key `cascade_game_v4`) is **discarded**, not migrated (#3697): every radius changed (Cherry 22 to 18, Watermelon 88 to 56, the capstone 101 to 63), so its saved positions would leave gaps the pile falls into, a different board from the one saved, built on the old physics. Like a v3 save it only exists in dev and test builds; its key is removed on load and on clear

## Scoring (Persistence)

- **Metric and direction:** `final_score`, higher is better, labelled `score` (`board` in `backend/cascade/module.py`; `BOARDS.cascade` in `frontend/src/api/vocab.ts`). Since #3700 it is the **merge score plus a win's time bonus**, computed by the server. The merge score is the points at game over: for each merge the engine (`engine.ts`) adds the new piece's `score` from `tiers.ts`.
- **Time bonus (#3700, owner decision 2026-11-16, "Balanced"; `B` raised by #3967, owner decision #3978).** `time_bonus = round(30000 × max(0, 1 − t / 360000))` for a win, 0 for a loss, where `t` is the completion's `duration_ms` (active play, see Duration). `B` = 30,000 (20,000 under rule 1), `T` = 6 min. Rounding is half up in integer arithmetic on both sides, so the app and the server agree to the point.
  - **Floor:** `MIN_WIN_MS` = 20 s. A win under it, or with no `duration_ms`, gets a bonus of 0. It is stored and ranks on its merge score; it is not rejected (a 400 dead-letters the game). The fastest possible win is well over it: on the 12-tier ladder a win takes at least about 135 perfect drops (27 s at the 200 ms drop cooldown, before any piece has fallen); the fastest skilled win in the #3967 runs took 182 s.
  - **Rule 2 (#3967):** on the 12-tier ladder the merge score grows about 58 points a second (35 on the 11-tier ladder), above rule 1's bonus slope of 56, and 100 of 145 bot wins (400 games, skilled and heuristic, 200 seeds each) scored below the best loss. With `B` = 30,000 (83 points a second) every win outranks every loss: lowest win total 23,777, best loss 23,406 (a 10-minute losing run), median win total 26,494 (median win 243 s, bonus 9,747). Rows scored under rule 1 keep `bonus_rule` 1.
  - **Why these numbers (rule 1):** sim of about 1,600 games on #3924 (#3700 comments). Merge score grows with game length at about 35 points/s, so the bonus slope (`B / T`, 56 points/s) has to beat it or slower wins outrank faster ones. With these numbers every bot win outranks every bot loss (median win total 16,402; best loss 12,094), halving the median win time is worth +5,872, and merge score still separates wins of the same pace. Revisit `T` against real `duration_ms` after launch.
  - **One source of truth:** `backend/cascade/time_bonus.py` (`BONUS_MAX`, `PAR_TIME_MS`, `MIN_WIN_MS` and the version `BONUS_RULE` = 2 since #3967). The server recomputes the bonus from the completion's `outcome` and `duration_ms` columns and ignores the app's copy. The app mirrors it for display only (`frontend/src/game/cascade/timeBonus.ts`). Both are checked against `backend/tests/fixtures/cascade_time_bonus.json` (`backend/tests/test_cascade_result.py`, `frontend/src/game/cascade/__tests__/timeBonus.test.ts`). A retune bumps `BONUS_RULE`, so rows scored under the old rule can be told apart without a migration.
  - **Old rows and old builds:** a completion without `merge_score` (a build before #3700) keeps the score it sent, with no bonus, and ranks by it. Abandons keep the score sent and never rank.
  - **Device clock (#3925):** the play clock measures with the monotonic `monotonicNow()` (`performance.now()`), not `Date.now()`, so setting the device clock back or forward mid-game does not change `t`. Only banked milliseconds (`playedMs`) are saved, never a clock reading.
- **Tie-break:** none declared (the research on #3700 dropped a `duration_ms` tie-break: boards don't support one without new board code and an index). Equal scores go to the entry the server received first (`received_at`), the last tie-break on every board.
- **Partitions:** none: one board.
- **Recorded, not partitioned:** the result block (see below). The fruit set is in the `game_started` event (`fruit_set`, `theme`), not in metadata.

| `final_score`                                         | server     | `merge_score + time_bonus` on a finished game (an abandon keeps the sent one). Equal to the `final_score` column.                                         |
| `duration_ms`, `theme`, `total_drops`, `total_merges` | app        | As before #3700. The `duration_ms` column is the authority.                                                                                               |

- `capstone` (the AC's name for the set id) is not sent: it duplicates `theme`.
- **Cost and volume (CLAUDE.md priorities):** about 100 bytes of JSON more per finished game (`merge_score`, `time_bonus`, `bonus_rule`, `max_tier`, `won`; `resultBlock.test.ts` keeps it under 120), on the game's existing row: no new rows, no per-event data, nothing uploaded during play (the block goes once at game end through `SyncWorker`). At 1,000 games a day that is about 100 KB a day, bounded by games played; Cascade is premium-only, so the volume tracks revenue. No dial or kill switch is needed for its size; it rides the existing result envelope.
- **Max value:** none (#2519 decision 14). Any integer up to 2³¹−1 is accepted.
- **Outcomes:** `has_winner = True` since #3699. Making the capstone records `win`; the lid's game over records `loss`. Builds before #3699 sent `completed` for both, which stays a finish with no winner. Restart during play records `abandoned` (`handleRestart`), and so does switching the fruit set mid-game (the fruit-set effect in `CascadeScreen.tsx` calls `endInstrumentedSession("abandoned")` and starts a new game). Leaving the screen records `abandoned` too (`useGameSync`'s unmount abandon), once a piece has been dropped. The Restart and fruit-set abandons carry the score so far in `final_score`, but abandoned rows never rank or count toward "best".
- **Duration:** Cascade's own `duration_ms` (`playedMs` in `CascadeScreen.tsx`): time since the game's first piece went in (a drop, or a test or dev placement; #3700, `startClockOnMove`; before it, since the session opened), less the time the player was away. A new game (mount, restart, fruit-set switch) starts with the clock not started, so time looking at an empty board doesn't count against the time bonus. The clock stops for good at the win or the lid, so the session and the card read the same time. Being away is another screen covering the board (navigation `blur`, #2743) or the app in the background (`AppState`, #2750): `usePauseWhileAway` stops the physics loop and the clock, a `PlayClock` (`startedAt` / `accumulatedMs`, `frontend/src/game/_shared/playClock.ts`) kept in a ref. Each animation frame carries the loop's generation, so a frame the OS delivers late after a trip to the background can't start a second loop. Because the value is > 0 it wins over `useGameSync`'s active-play window. The saved board carries `playedMs` (`SavedState` in `frontend/src/game/cascade/storage.ts`), and the pause saves the board, so a game continued after an app kill keeps the play before it and doesn't count the time the app was closed. A save from a build before #2750 has no `playedMs`: its earlier play is unknown, so the game counts from the relaunch.
- **How it reaches the server:** the `useGameRecorder("cascade")` session row (`useGameSync` plus the replay recorder, #3858). `SyncWorker` sends `POST /games` after the first drop (`markStarted`) and `PATCH /games/{id}/complete` at game over. If the player has a display name (`PUT /players/me`), the row ranks with no further step. The board shows each named player's best game once. Shared rules: [Leaderboard routes](../GAME-CONTRACT.md#leaderboard-routes-2618).
- **Where the player sees it:** the result card shows the rank through `lookupGameRank` (`GET /games/{id}/rank`), or asks once for a display name. The card's "View leaderboard" link and the ⋯ menu open the Leaderboard screen (#2633). Stats (#2635) are in the ⋯ menu: the shared Stats screen shows Cascade's wins, losses, win rate and win streaks (`has_winner`) and Best (the highest score, bonus included). A "Fastest Win" tile (m:ss) shows `best_win_ms` (#3927): the minimum `duration_ms` over `win` rows, ignoring wins under `MIN_WIN_MS` (20 s); the tile is hidden when it is null. `best_win_ms` is client-reported (`duration_ms`) and protected only by the per-game floor; it is private to `/stats/me`, and needs a server-side bound before it is ever shown to other players or used on a board. The time of each win is also in `duration_ms` and on the win card. Store builds hide Cascade (`HIDDEN_GAMES`, `frontend/src/entitlements/gameVisibility.ts`), so there it has no leaderboard or stats entry point.

## Replay recording (#3858)

Cascade records its play on the shared `GameRecorder` (`frontend/src/game/_shared/gameRecorder.ts`, epic #3273), through `useGameRecorder("cascade")` in `useCascadeSession.ts`, so a game can be read back from a Sentry issue (`game.id`) and tuned from real games. The format and the log are `frontend/src/game/cascade/replay.ts`.

**What is recorded.** No positions per frame and no board snapshots.

| Event              | Data                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `game_started` (L) | `fruit_set`, `theme` (the piece set), `seed` (the queue's), `engine_seed` (the drop nudge's), `tuning` (`CASCADE_TUNING_ID`, the physics tuning: #3697's lively tuning, #3904's overlap tuning, #3920's ladder), `engine_version` (`ENGINE_VERSION` in `engine.ts`, 1). No app version yet: pending #3942, for every game (the golden fixture, recorded before that, has an `app_version`, read as optional) |
| `drop` (R)         | One per unit: `d`, the unit's drops, each `[step, x_tenths, tier]`; `z`, its danger moments, each `[start, peak]`; `rs: 1` on the first unit after a restored game. Never split                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| `merge` (R)        | One per unit: `m`, the unit's merges, each `[step, tierFrom, chain]`; split into parts on `m` if over its cap                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| `game_ended` (L)   | #3700's result block (score, merge score, time bonus, outcome, `duration_ms`, drops, merges, top tier) plus `end_step`, `capstone_step` (a win), `lid_step` (the first step the lid's count ran), `rec_cut` / `z_cut` (units or danger moments left out past a cap) and the recorder's `replay` summary                                                                                                      |

- **`step`** is the engine's fixed-step index (`CascadeEngine.steps`, 60 a second, the same at 60 and 120 Hz), never wall time. A merge event carries the step it happened in.
- **`x_tenths`**: the screen drops at `quantizeX(x)`, the tap's x to a tenth of a world pixel, so the recorded integer replays to exactly the x the engine got. The engine's own seeded nudge (±0.4 px) comes on top, from `engine_seed`.
- **`chain`** counts the merges since the last drop, from 1 (the screen's chain link).
- **Danger moments** are the engine's `danger` events: the lid's overflow count ran from fixed step `start` and reached `peak` of 180 steps before it reset, or the game ended. Flickers under 10 steps (`DANGER_MIN_TICKS`) are not kept, and at most 32 a game (`CASCADE_MAX_DANGER`).
- **Units**: one per 30 drops (`CASCADE_DROPS_PER_UNIT`; 20 before #3967, owner decision #3978: fuller rows cut the stored bytes and the storage writes with no data lost; a crash can lose up to 30 drops), or fewer when the game ends or the app goes to the background. Merges go with the unit open when they happen.

**Performance.** Nothing runs per frame or on the physics step: the engine adds one step counter and a step field on its merge events, and emits a `danger` event only when an episode ends. The screen's drop pushes one tuple into the log; the loop hands each step's events to `rec.events`, which does nothing for an empty list and pushes a tuple per merge. Every 30 drops the log hands its unit to the recorder (`beginUnit`, two `record`s, `closeUnit`), which measures and saves it at the next idle moment, in one storage write. Going to the background (`useFlushOnBackground`) hands over the unit waiting and saves it at once; an idle save may never come before the OS kills the app. Every end hands over the last unit before `game_ended`, after flushing the danger moment still running (`CascadeEngine.flushDanger()`): the win, the loss, a New Game and leaving the screen. Leaving the screen is the hook's own abandon, whose `game_ended` gets the end, capstone and lid steps through the progress snapshot's `payload` (`useGameSync`), never the PATCH result. Gameplay rows are not uploaded during play: SyncWorker holds them until the session ends, the app goes to the background, or the 5-minute fallback (#3275).

**Replays.** Physics replays bit for bit only on one JS engine (see [Physics](#physics)), so the merges and the outcome are recorded to tell the game's story on their own. `backend/cascade/replay.py` reads it for `backend/scripts/replay_game.py` (#3277): the seeds and tuning, drops and merges by tier, the longest chain, the danger moments, the outcome with its end and capstone steps, the score with its time bonus, and a check that the recorded merges add up to the merge score (a merge into tier n scores 2^n). Nothing on the server re-simulates a game, and nothing may verify a score that way. `rebuild.ts` re-simulates a recorded game from its seed and drops as a best-effort extra (a restored game, `rs`, is not re-simulated: its steps restart on a restored board). The golden replay (`__tests__/replay.fixture.test.ts`, V8) rebuilds a seeded real-engine game from `backend/tests/fixtures/cascade_replay_v1.json` alone and requires its hand-written score (18,862), merges (242) and outcome (the win at step 32,799; re-recorded on the 12-tier ladder, #3967 / #3975, owner decision #3974: 6,430, 132 and step 17,450 before); the backend test (`test_cascade_replay_events.py`) validates every event of the same fixture and reads its story. The fixture is pinned by the replay fixture guard (`.github/replay-fixtures.json`, #3911): a change to the physics, the ladder, the queue or the recording that moves it bumps `ENGINE_VERSION` and keeps the old fixture (#3908).

**Volume and caps (CLAUDE.md: cost, then performance, then data).** Measured on seeded real-engine games (`replay.bench.test.ts`, `replay.budget.test.ts`):

| Game                                         | Rows | JSON on the device and uploaded | Stored (`jsonb::text` + 200 B a row) |
| -------------------------------------------- | ---- | ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| A typical game (about 5.5 min, ~255 drops)   | 18   | ~6.5 KB                         | ~12 KB                               |
| The golden win (9.1 min, 271 drops)          | 20   | 7.4 KB                          | 13.1 KB                              |
| A 10-minute game (249 drops, 210 merges)     | 18   | 6.7 KB                          | ~11.7 KB                             |
| The most a game can record (32 units, worst) | 64   | ~32 KB                          | 60.4 KB                              |

(#3967: the 12-tier ladder with 30-drop units. On the 11-tier ladder with 20-drop units: a typical game 18 rows and 9.5 KB stored, the golden win 16 rows and 8.2 KB, the 10-minute game 26 rows and 13.8 KB, the worst 96 rows and 66.9 KB.)

- A drop entry is about 15 B and a merge about 12 B; with the envelope, about 28 B of JSON a drop. The widest rows seen are 336 B (`drop`) and 324 B (`merge`).
- **Caps** (`eventQueueConfig.ts`, held equal by `test_cascade_replay_events.py`): 1 KB per `drop` event (never split) and per `merge` event (split on `m`), 32 KB of JSON a recorder session (`CASCADE_SESSION_MAX_BYTES`, about half an hour), 32 units a game (`CASCADE_MAX_UNITS`, 960 drops; 48 units of 20 before #3967; saved with the game, so a relaunch does not reset it), 32 danger moments a game. Past a cap the game records nothing more of it, as a clean prefix, and says so: the recorder's one throttled Sentry report (counts only) and `x` in the `game_ended` replay summary, and `rec_cut` / `z_cut` in `game_ended`. The server holds a game to `CASCADE_CAP`, 128 events and 80 KB (`backend/games/replay_caps.py`), and drops a bad event (`cascade/events.py`).
- **Per day:** about 12.3 KB stored a game when its replay uploads, lifecycle rows included (the budget day's three games: 36,879 B). At the sample rate `r` (the catalog's `replay.sample_rate`; 10 % when the app has no dial): **1,000 Cascade games a day store about 12.3 MB × r a day, about 370 MB × r at the 30-day retention steady state** (37 MB at 10 %; 285 MB × r on the 11-tier ladder: the 12-tier games are longer). The heavy player's day in the budget test (games of about 5.5 and 5.4 minutes and one of 10) stores 36.9 KB, inside the 39,492 B budget. Cascade is premium-only, so the volume tracks paying players.
- **Dials** (no release needed): the kill switch (`game_types.replay_enabled` / `REPLAY_RECORDING_ENABLED`: the recorder records nothing, only the lifecycle rows go), the sample rate (a sampled-out session's `drop` / `merge` rows stay on the device and are deleted there at its end), the upgrade on error, and the 90 % storage trip that sets every rate to 0 (#3279, #3292). Tested in `__tests__/recording.failsafes.test.ts` (kill switch, 0 % and 100 %, an oversized session cut and flagged) and `backend/tests/test_replay_failsafes.py`.
- **Sentry:** a Sentry event during a Cascade game carries `game.id` and `game.type` (#3277, `sessionTags.ts`; `__tests__/useCascadeSession.recording.test.tsx`).
- **Test seams** (test builds): `__cascade_dropAt` and `__cascade_spawnTierAt` drop through the screen's recording, at the same tenth-of-a-pixel x. `__cascade_setSeed` replaces the game's seeds: before the first drop the game restarts with them, so its `game_started` names them; after it the next unit is marked `rs` (not re-simulable from `game_started`). The demo set-ups restore a pile the recording does not see, and the dev panel drops without the recorder: a game played from either is not re-simulable.
