# Round 26 — Fresh compositional foundation

**Status: rejected as a suitable Star Swarm foundation after owner audition (November 11, 2026).**

After the rejected Round 24/25 efforts, this round composes the entire musical bed **from silence** rather than mixing new instruments over the previous slow atmospheric recording.

## Files
- `starswarm_round26_fresh_score.py`: deterministic Python synthesis generator; uses numpy and ffmpeg; **does not read any older Star Swarm MP3**, copyrighted reference track, or third-party sample
- `starswarm-round26-A-cinematic-resolve.mp3`: 1:05 original composition with a clear forward melodic statement, answering phrase, changing bass motion and occasional sparse high-register responses
- `starswarm-round26-B-swaying-pursuit.mp3`: same underlying original melodic/harmonic structure, with the line passed between horn-like and bowed voices, subtle contrary motion, and a slightly more flowing feel

Both ~65.1s, stereo 24 kHz, 128 kbps MP3. Integrated loudness measured A ~−19.2 LUFS, B ~−19.5 LUFS. Neither incorporates the previously approved feature effects (Shepard descents, rising astronomical tones, choruses or robotic signals). Those are intentionally held back until the independent core musical score is accepted.

## Audition question
Does an ordinary one-minute passage now stand up *as music*: coherent, dark, science-fiction-coded, purposeful and energized enough to support nonstop combat? Do the instruments and harmony create forward motion without external ambient-bed clutter? A favors an exposed melodic lead; B favors flowing distribution of the same line. Compare with original BG-1/BG-2 at perceptually matched loudness; also check with gameplay SFX. If neither succeeds, **do not extend to a full-length master**.

## Owner feedback after listening

The first reaction to both arrangements was **"absolutely not"**: they evoke **medieval/fantasy** music rather than science fiction. There is **some genuine improvement in forward momentum and excitement**, but still **not enough**, and the change sacrifices the space identity. Neither version is approved. Do not treat increased pace alone as success.

The owner clarified a key part of original **BG-1's first seconds**: the music has a forceful, cinematic **arrival immediately**, somewhat analogous to the grand opening *effect* of a space-opera score such as Star Wars. This is a reference to **entrance energy, drama, scale and confidence**, **not** a request to copy the Star Wars theme, melody, orchestration or recognizable musical phrases.

### Next-round direction
- Compose a *new coherent musical foundation*, not additional overlays on earlier ambient audio.
- Avoid exposed, conventional horn/bowed-ensemble figures that suggest a medieval/fantasy quest; Round 26's waveform and phrasing made this association too salient.
- Keep the modest compositional momentum gain, but bring back original project's **inharmonic, unstable, electromagnetic and expansive sci-fi timbre**, without falling back on the stagnant old ambient bed.
- **First 0–5 seconds:** immediate musical statement/arrival, not a gradual tranquil fade-in. **First 5–20 seconds:** meaningful development and a convincing sci-fi identity. Maintain an original melody/harmonic trajectory and a serious mission-driven tone.
- Compare an unadorned 45–65 second bed with BG-1's *perceived urgency and structural confidence*, not its specific notes; evaluate during continuous game combat.
- Still hold back all already-approved feature effects (Shepard, astronomical rise, choruses, organ, robotic signals) until the underlying score works independently.

Keep Round 26 files as rejected reference material; continue pushing experiments to this same draft PR #3734.
